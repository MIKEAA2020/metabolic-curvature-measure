Dear Editors,

I am pleased to submit my manuscript "A Geometric and
Category-Theoretic Theory of Viability: How Sequential Adaptations
Induce Path-Dependent Risk" for consideration as a Research Article in
Discover Applied Mathematics, via the journal's Snapp submission
system.

The paper develops, from the ground up, an applied-mathematical
framework for a problem that arises when many individually manageable
changes accumulate into a viability threat: non-commuting sequences of
perturbations and the policy holonomy they induce. Every claim is
either proved, reduced to cited theory, marked as a proof sketch, or
machine-verified by committed scripts — and the framework is applied
throughout to metabolic networks at genome scale. The development has
four layers, each stated with its proof status explicitly labeled:

1. **The SAVGS object and the 2-category StCon(B).** A stratified
   G_C-reduced bundle assembles a control base, a policy bundle with
   Fisher–Rao geometry, a viability margin, a maintenance graph, and a
   boundary span; the 2-category of stratified connections carries a
   lax-functorial gluing theorem and a piecewise holonomy formula,
   with the small-loop expansion stated for loops that cross a
   constraint-switching wall transversally in pairs, at the true order
   of the boundary term, in agreement with machine-verified numerics.

2. **A single composition theorem for heterogeneous domain bridges.**
   Seven bridges are typed as optics; the composed update is realized
   on a complete metric state space, with a per-optic Lipschitz
   product bound and, for an explicit seven-map instantiation, a
   Banach contraction of the Krasnoselskii–Mann-averaged update. The
   projected CPTP channel contraction settles the Zeno self-reference
   resolution.

3. **A filtered-colimit construction of RAF sets** in Optic(Set),
   proved at the Set level with an adapter-level optic statement and
   verified at scale, with a three-phase autopoiesis closure test; its
   external anchor on the Keio single-gene-deletion collection
   (transitive, direct, and cross-rebuild arms, with a disclosed
   medium audit, its glucose-only re-run: both in-silico
   essentiality sets invariant, every rank-level association
   strengthened, and the negative cross-rebuild verdict reversed as a
   medium artifact; four further perturbation probes extending the
   label-invariance claim across the oxygen, nitrogen, phosphate, and
   iron axes -- the labels invariant under supply limitations up to a
   four-fold oxygen reduction and 89% growth reduction in both
   reconstructions, re-stratifying only at the anaerobic regime
   switch and at nitrogen-source substitution (losses confined to the
   rewired assimilation module, every rescue
   substitution-mediated); the reported association statistics
   homogenized under canonical (parsimonious) vertex selection, with
   the plain-FBA collapses diagnosed as flux-solution degeneracy by
   at-optimum variability analysis; a sixth, non-medium probe on the
   ATP-maintenance axis and an independent-engine audit (24,282
   gene-level comparisons) that surfaced and corrected
   solver-tolerance artifacts in trace-quota essentiality calls);
   its viability-kernel reading as a finite-time,
   feedback-certifying probe of kernel and capture-basin membership
   (with the Nagumo tangentiality condition); the Poincare/averaging
   analysis of its pathwise levels; and a benchmark against the
   structural closure instruments of chemical-organization theory
   and network expansion.

4. **A homotopy-type-theoretic extension** (the homotopy fixed-point
   construction), with its proof-sketch status explicitly marked as
   such.

Proof discipline is the paper's organizing principle: complete proofs;
proofs that reduce to cited descent/coherence theory (marked); proof
sketches (marked); and machine-verified statements whose scripts and
artifacts are committed. Conjectures stay conjectures. Adjacent
formalizations are cited at their sources (categorical autopoiesis;
chemical-organization theory; network expansion; contact-geometric
Noether theory; open-system Zeno dynamics; algorithmic rate
distortion). The paper is accordingly applied mathematics in the
journal's sense: a constructive geometric framework, quantitative
theorems with constants, and terminating machine experiments that
could refute each claim — carried out on public genome-scale
metabolic models (iJO1366 and iML1515) with every artifact deposited.

**Disclosure of a related manuscript.** In the interest of full
transparency I disclose that a companion application manuscript is in
preparation for separate submission: "A discrete curvature measure for
flux balance analysis predicts transcriptional regulation in
*Escherichia coli*." The division of labor is deliberate:

- The **submitted manuscript** develops the full geometric and
  categorical theory: all definitions, constructions, theorems, and
  their proofs or marked proof sketches, together with the machine
