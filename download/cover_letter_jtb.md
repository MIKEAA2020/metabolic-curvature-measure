Dear Editors,

I am pleased to submit my manuscript "A Geometric Theory of Metabolic
Flux Rerouting: How Active-Set Curvature Predicts Transcriptional
Regulation and Protein-Layer Buffering" for consideration as an
Original Research Article in the Journal of Theoretical Biology. The
manuscript sits squarely in the journal's regulatory-flux-balance
lineage (Covert, Schilling & Palsson, JTB 213:73–88, 2001, and its
descendants): it asks what the optimal flux map of a genome-scale
metabolic model does under continuously varying nutrients and enzyme
capacities, and what the transcriptional response to that geometry is.

Parametric flux balance analysis defines a piecewise-affine map from
environmental and genetic parameters to optimal metabolic fluxes. The
manuscript establishes that the distributional second derivative of
this map is a matrix-valued Radon measure concentrated on the
codimension-one strata of the active-set complex — a discrete
curvature carrier — and that this measure organizes the network's
rerouting geometry. Four results make the work suitable for the
journal's readership:

1. **A theory with a proved refinement bridge.** The atomic measure
   converges weakly to the smooth curvature density under mesh
   refinement, with a strong $L^1$ reconstruction law and an exact
   counterexample calculus delimiting total-variation convergence
   (Theorem B'). The value layer (biomass objective) is provably
   tie-break-free; loop holonomy scales linearly in loop size —
   slope 1.000000 — where any smooth map would scale quadratically.
   A hand-checkable worked example (three chambers, two walls, one
   growth-silent) lets a reader verify every object of the theory by
   arithmetic, and a metabolic-control-analysis comparison
   (Kacser–Burns; Heinrich–Rapoport) positions the framework against
   the local-sensitivity tradition the journal's readers know well.

2. **A validated, robustly tested gene-sensitivity metric.** From the
   measure the manuscript derives a per-gene sensitivity score and
   shows, in *E. coli* (M3D microarray compendium, n = 424 genes),
   that it predicts carbon-depletion transcriptional response
   (r = +0.395, p = 2.6 × 10⁻¹⁷; partial r = +0.269). The association
   survives every robustness check in the manuscript: metric invariance
   (ρ = 0.99998), five lexicographic tie-break conventions with the
   value layer exactly invariant, path variation, cross-platform
   replication (dissociating on PRECISE, as the layer theory predicts),
   and Glivenko–Cantelli stabilization of the underlying event measures
   across random panels. The association does not propagate to the
   protein layer (proteome r = −0.083 vs. transcript +0.419): cells
   transcribe the potential for rerouting while buffering translation.

3. **Named biology.** The induced genes sit in eleven enriched regulons
   led by CRP (Benjamini–Hochberg q = 8.6 × 10⁻¹⁶) at whole-operon
   granularity (nuo, atp, sdh–suc, cyo); the rerouting mass
   concentrates on twenty named branch metabolites of central carbon
   (59.7% of mass, led by F6P, GAP, G6P, DHAP, PEP), giving the theory
   a targeted-metabolomics readout; the buffering null is developed
   into a quantitative deduction that metabolic memory must live in
   fast post-translational state; and a construction-order design rule
   for strain engineering is derived from the same geometry
   (ρ = 0.442, sign agreement 59/66, binomial p = 2.4 × 10⁻¹¹).

4. **Architecture.** The walls of the flux geometry sit on the
   conserved, internal, branch-point chemistry of central carbon
   metabolism while the exchange interface stays flat — a design
   principle that explains the conservation of central branch
   chemistry alongside the divergence of its transcriptional wiring,
   with a cross-species prediction stated as a prediction.

The mathematics is deliberately classical — parametric linear
programming (chamber complexes, active sets, Danskin duality),
Alexandrov's theorem, and distributional second derivatives of
piecewise-affine maps — applied systematically to flux-balance models.
The computational work is fully reproducible: every figure and number
in the manuscript is regenerated by committed scripts from deposited
artifacts, verified by a 344-check numeric audit (344/344 PASS) that
re-derives each manuscript value from its committed artifact. All
code, data manifests (SHA-256), and artifacts are publicly available;
all biological data are public (BiGG models, M3D, PRECISE, PaxDb, and
the condition-dependent *E. coli* proteome). No open-access charges
are requested; publication under the journal's standard route is
intended.
