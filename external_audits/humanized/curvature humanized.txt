qwen:
\paragraph{The object.}
Consider a metabolic network with \(m\) reactions, modeled by flux balance analysis
\citep{varma1994, orth2010}. Environmental or genetic perturbations are represented by
parameters \(\theta \in \Theta \subset \RR^p\), such as uptake bounds or knockdown
scalings, and these parameters enter the constraints affinely. The optimal flux map
\[
v : \Theta \to \RR^m
\]
is continuous and piecewise affine by parametric linear programming
\citep{borrelli2003}. Its breakpoints occur on the active-set strata of the constraint
complex, that is, on the loci where the set of binding constraints changes. Therefore,
the distributional second derivative
\[
\mu = D^2 v
\]
is not an ordinary function. It is a measure: the discrete curvature carrier of the
network’s rerouting geometry.

\paragraph{The claim structure.}
This manuscript establishes the following results, in order.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item It characterizes the atomic structure of \(\mu_h\) and proves its weak convergence
      under refinement (Theorem~\ref{thm:Bprime}, \S\ref{sec:theoremB}). An exact
      counterexample calculus separates what is provable --- weak convergence and strong
      cell-wise reconstruction --- from what fails in general, namely total-variation
      convergence.
\item It distinguishes two geometric layers. The codimension-one Hessian layer is the
      unbiased curvature estimator. The codimension-two angle-defect layer converges to
      the Gauss-map area density, with an exact sec-law bias
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:seclaw}).
\item It establishes a regime dichotomy. Piecewise-affine maps with fixed kink complexes
      produce \(O(\varepsilon)\) loop holonomy, with measured slope \(1.00\), whereas
      smooth maps produce \(O(\varepsilon^2)\).
\item It identifies the value function
      \[
      \Phi = c_{\mathrm{bio}}^\top v^*
      \]
      as the canonical tie-break-free carrier. Shadow-price jumps are measurable to
      \(6\)--\(7\) digits against Danskin envelopes.
\item It shows that the measure-derived metric \(\kmu\) predicts carbon-depletion
      transcriptional response (\(r = +0.395\)); generalizes to oxygen-limitation and
      carbon-switch trajectories with matched responses (\(r = +0.32\), \(+0.22\);
      \S\ref{sec:v7}); and is robust to the tie-break convention, with
      \(r \in [+0.386, +0.396]\) across five selection rules
      (\S\ref{sec:v8}). The association is buffered at the protein layer
      (\S\ref{sec:empirical}), and the associated event measures stabilize at the
      Glivenko--Cantelli rate across random panels (\S\ref{sec:e32}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Positioning.}
\citet{kochanowski2013} showed that global metabolic coordination under catabolic
limitation is largely passive at the protein and metabolite levels, being driven mainly
by Crp and local metabolite concentrations. Our question is different. We ask whether a
gene-specific, network-derived measure of rerouting necessity predicts transcriptional
response. We find that it does. We also find that this transcript-level association does
not propagate to the protein layer. This is consistent with a model in which cells
transcribe the potential for rerouting while buffering its translation.

\section{The discrete curvature measure of parametric FBA}
\label{sec:measure}

\subsection{The atomic curvature measure}

The following definition makes the intuitive object precise: curvature is concentrated
where the slope of the piecewise-affine map jumps.

\begin{definition}[Atomic curvature measure]\label{def:mu}
Let \(u : \Omega \to \RR^m\) be continuous and piecewise affine on a polyhedral complex
\(\CC\) covering \(\Omega\). For each codimension-one facet \(F\) of \(\CC\), shared by
cells \(\tau^-\) and \(\tau^+\), let
\[
[\nabla u]_F = \nabla u|_{\tau^+} - \nabla u|_{\tau^-}
\]
be the jump in the gradient across \(F\), let \(n_F\) be the unit conormal oriented from
\(\tau^-\) to \(\tau^+\), and let \(|F|\) denote the facet measure. The
\emph{atomic curvature measure} is the matrix-valued Radon measure
\[
\mu \;=\; D^2 u \;=\; \sum_{F} A_F \, \delta_F,
\qquad
A_F \;=\; [\nabla u]_F \otimes n_F \, |F|
\;\in\; \RR^{m \times p \times p}.
\]
Thus each facet contributes an atom whose magnitude is the gradient jump, whose
orientation is given by the conormal, and whose weight is the facet area.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Support]\label{rem:support}
The measure \(\mu\) is concentrated on the \((p-1)\)-skeleton. The \((p-2)\)-skeleton
carries a different object, namely the angle-defect layer
(Proposition~\ref{prop:seclaw}); the two layers should not be conflated. In the
one-dimensional case \(p=1\), the atoms reduce to the familiar slope jumps at
breakpoints.
\end{remark}

The measure-theoretic language used here is classical. Matrix-valued Radon measures and
their weak convergence are treated in \citet{billingsley1999}; the Alexandrov Hessian of
convex functions in \citet{alexandrov1939}; the Monge--Amp\`ere measure in
\citet{gutierrez2001}; and Regge-type defect measures for piecewise-flat curvature in
\citet{regge1961} and \citet{cheeger1984}. On the polyhedral side, the relevant objects
include chamber complexes, normal fans, and multi-parametric linear-programming value
functions \citep{rockafellar1970, ziegler1995, borrelli2003}. The
Kantorovich--Rubinstein, or flat, norm used in the refinement--resolution bridge is the
standard optimal-transport flat norm \citep{villani2009}.

\subsection{The measure-theoretic sensitivity metric}

To connect the curvature measure with gene-level prediction, we integrate its mass along
a one-dimensional parameter trajectory.

\begin{definition}[$\kmu$]\label{def:kmu}
Let \(\theta(t)\), \(t \in [0,T]\), be a \(C^1\) parameter trajectory, and let
\(v(\theta(t))\) be the corresponding piecewise-affine optimal-flux response. Let
\(\{t_e\}_e\) be the event times at which the trajectory crosses active-set strata, and
let \(A_e = A_{F(e)}\) be the atom evaluated on the crossed facet. Define
\[
\kmu(g)
\;=\;
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{e} \bigl\| A_e \bigr\| \, w_e(g),
\]
where \(\|{\cdot}\|\) is the entrywise \(1\)-norm on
\(\RR^{m \times p \times p}\), and \(w_e(g) \in \{0,1\}\) selects the events whose
crossed stratum changes the activity of reactions assigned to gene \(g\), taking the
maximum over the reactions associated with that gene according to the GPR rules.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[$\kmu$ lies in the safe regime of the reconstruction]
The metric \(\kmu\) integrates measure mass along a one-dimensional trajectory. This is
the telescoping regime in which total variation and mass are automatically consistent
(see \S\ref{sec:theoremB}). Its resolution independence is exactly the
\(L^1\)-reconstruction regime of Theorem~\ref{thm:Bprime}(iv); numerically, the measured
mass \(288.77\) is stable under \(4\times\) and \(8\times\) trajectory refinement. The
per-reaction masses are the components of the flux-layer crease measure whose objective
contraction is \(D^2\Phi\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:coupling}). In this sense, the metric is
the primal layer of the canonical object.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Fixed definition, tie-breaking, and layer assignment]
\label{rem:lock}
For the empirical claims of this manuscript, the definition of \(\kmu\) is fixed as
follows.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
\item The carrier is the flux-layer signed crease measure
      \(D^2 v^*\), obtained by applying Definition~\ref{def:mu} to
      \(u = v^*\), where \(v^*\) is the three-stage lexicographic optimal flux
      (\S\ref{sec:methods}). The lexicographic tie-break --- biomass first, then
      \(\ell_1\) flux, then seeded deterministic weights --- is part of the metric’s
      definition, not an implementation detail. The flux layer is selection-dependent by
      construction, and the tie-break is therefore declared rather than eliminated.
\item The per-reaction mass is the unsigned total variation
      \[
      \frac{1}{T}\sum_e \|A_e\|_1
      \]
      along the parameter trajectory.
\item The value layer \(D^2\Phi\) enters only as a derived diagnostic. By
      Theorem~\ref{thm:coupling},
      \[
      D^2\Phi = \sum_r c_r\, D^2 v^*_r.
      \]
      It is not used as the predictor. No claim in this manuscript attributes
      transcriptional response to Alexandrov curvature of the value function.
\item The layer correlated with transcriptomics is the codimension-one Hessian, or
      crease, layer of the flux map. It is not the Monge--Amp\`ere atom layer
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:maatom}), which requires concavity and serves instead as a
      dual-face diagnostic.
\end{enumerate}

Robustness to the declared selection is measured, not assumed. The within-layer rank
identity is
\[
\rho(\kmu, \kk^{\mathrm{lex}}) = 0.99998
\]
(\S\ref{sec:v5}). The trajectory substitutions of \S\ref{sec:v7} reproduce the
association under the same declared selection on two additional paths. In
\S\ref{sec:v8}, the association remains stable, with
\[
r \in [+0.386, +0.396]
\]
and partial correlation constant to three decimals, under four alternative stage-3
selection rules, while the value layer remains exactly invariant. Including the declared
rule, the association is therefore stable across five selection rules.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The value-function layer (Alexandrov)}

Although the flux map depends on the choice of optimal representative, the value function
is selection-free. The following results describe its measure-theoretic structure.

\begin{proposition}[Existence and tie-break-freeness]\label{prop:alex}
Let
\[
\Phi(\theta)
=
\max\{ c^\top v : Sv = 0,\ \ell(\theta) \le v \le u(\theta)\},
\]
with \(\ell\) and \(u\) affine, and assume the problem is feasible and bounded. Then
\(\Phi\) is concave, being the pointwise infimum of affine dual objectives over a
\(\theta\)-independent dual feasible set. Consequently, \(-\Phi\) carries a symmetric
matrix-valued Radon Hessian measure \(D^2(-\Phi)\) in the sense of Alexandrov
\citep{alexandrov1939}, independently of the optimal-flux selection rule.

Concavity fails in general under OR, or isoenzyme, GPR rules for simultaneous
multi-gene capacity vectors. Single-gene axes, exchange-bound families, and AND-only
subnetworks retain concavity. For OR-containing rules, the value function remains
continuous and piecewise affine and carries the signed crease measure of
Proposition~\ref{prop:semiconvex}; only the Monge--Amp\`ere layer requires the concave
class.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Two-layer structure of \(D^2(-\Phi)$]\label{prop:twolayer}
Let \(f\) be convex and piecewise affine. Then its second derivative separates into two
geometric layers.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
\item The singular part of \(D^2 f\) is supported on codimension-one facets. Its density
      is
      \[
      (g^{+} - g^{-}) \otimes n
      =
      [\partial_{n} f]\, n \otimes n
      \]
      per unit facet measure, where \(g^\pm\) are the one-sided gradients. This layer has
      no point atoms at codimension-two vertices: the mass in an \(\varepsilon\)-neighborhood
      of such a vertex is \(O(\varepsilon)\). The measured log--log slope is \(1.000\).
\item The Monge--Amp\`ere measure \(\det D^2 f\) is atomic at codimension-two vertices.
      Each atom equals the volume of the normal fan,
      \[
      \bigl|\operatorname{conv}\{\nabla f(\tau) : \tau \ni \text{vertex}\}\bigr|.
      \]
      In numerical checks, the atom remains constant at \(0.5\) under
      \(\varepsilon\)-shrinking.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Dual optimal face identity]\label{prop:dualface}
At a codimension-two vertex \(\theta_v\) of a parametric-LP value function, the
Monge--Amp\`ere atom of \(-\Phi\) equals the area of the normal fan, and this fan is the
image of the LP dual optimal face under \(\theta \mapsto U^\top y\):
\[
\det D^2(-\Phi)(\{\theta_v\})
=
\bigl|\operatorname{conv}\{\nabla C_i\}\bigr|
=
\bigl|\{U^\top y : (\lambda, y) \text{ optimal dual}\}\bigr|.
\]

This identity was machine-verified on a two-parameter max-flow LP, which serves as a
phenotype phase plane in miniature. The vertex was refined to machine precision by an
exact triple-tie solve; \(\Phi(\theta_v)\) matched the LP value to \(10^{-15}\); and the
fan area was \(0.235\), compared with \(0.235000068\) from direct enumeration of the
dual optimal face using \(16\) direction LPs.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Layer separation and phenotype phase-plane positioning]
\label{rem:layersep}
The phenotype phase planes of \citet{edwards2001} and \citet{ibarra2002} are the chamber
complex of \(\Phi\); their sector corners are the atoms of the Monge--Amp\`ere layer.
The empirical metric \(\kmu\) of Definition~\ref{def:kmu}, however, is a functional of
the flux-map measure \(D^2 v\), not of the value function alone. This flux-layer measure
is tie-break-sensitive. In a controlled follower-variable LP, two lexicographic
tie-breaks leave the value layer invariant while moving the flux layer’s
second-difference mass by ten orders of magnitude (\S\ref{sec:m1}). The controlled
relation between the value layer and the flux layer is now a theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:coupling} and Corollary~\ref{cor:valueflux}). The association results
of \S\ref{sec:empirical} stand on the flux layer alone, because that is where the
evidence in \S\ref{sec:v5} and \S\ref{sec:v6} actually resides.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The value--flux coupling}

\begin{lemma}[Lex-selection regularity]\label{lem:lex}
Let \(\ell\) and \(u\) be continuous and piecewise affine, and let \(v^*(\theta)\) be the
three-stage lexicographic optimal flux (\S\ref{sec:methods}). Then \(v^*\) is well
defined, continuous, and piecewise affine. Its graph over each GPR cell is a finite
polyhedral complex in \((v,\theta)\)-space: each lexicographic stage adjoins the graph of
a piecewise-affine value function, which is polyhedral, and an injective polyhedral
projection is piecewise affine. Numerically, path-segment residuals are at most
\(8 \times 10^{-14}\) (\S\ref{sec:m1}), and \(\Phi\) is piecewise affine to
\(4.2 \times 10^{-13}\) (\S\ref{sec:v1}).
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Value--flux crease coupling]\label{thm:coupling}
Since
\[
\Phi = c^\top v^*
\]
pointwise, the corresponding symmetric matrix-valued signed Radon measures satisfy
\[
D^2\Phi
=
\sum_{r} c_r\, D^2 v^*_r .
\]
Along any piecewise-affine trajectory, at every crease time \(t_k\),
\[
\Delta\Phi'(t_k) = c^\top \Delta v'(t_k),
\]
and
\[
|\Delta\Phi'| \le \|c\|_\infty \,\|\Delta v'\|_1.
\]

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Visibility dichotomy.} An event with unique optima on both sides is a
      transversal optimal-vertex switch and is necessarily objective-moving.
      Objective-invisible events, defined by
      \[
      c^\top \Delta v' = 0
      \quad\text{and}\quad
      \Delta v' \neq 0,
      \]
      are exactly the degenerate reroutings inside optimal faces of dimension at least
      one; these are mask-type events. The tie-break sensitivity of the flux layer is
      concentrated in the invisible events. The \(c\)-contraction is tie-break-free
      because it annihilates the degenerate layer.
\item \textbf{Sparse-objective corollary.} If
      \[
      c = \gamma e_{\mathrm{bio}},
      \]
      then
      \[
      D^2\Phi = \gamma\, D^2 v^*_{\mathrm{bio}}.
      \]
      Thus the value layer is the crease measure of the single biomass component, and
      per-gene attribution from the value layer alone is structurally impossible.
\end{enumerate}

The identity was machine-verified with error \(0.0\) at all \(12\) events and all
\(4\) clusters of the iML1515 cut, with \(\Phi = c^\top v^*\) to \(10^{-9}\)
(Fig.~\ref{fig:coupling}). Eleven of the twelve events were objective-invisible, with
flux \(\ell_1\) slope jumps up to \(1.6 \times 10^{4}\), while
\[
|c^\top \Delta v'| \le 1.51 \times 10^{-7}.
\]
In \(150\) random dense-objective LPs, the identity held to \(1.1 \times 10^{-13}\); the
five observed events were all objective-moving, illustrating the dichotomy. The
degenerate follower family was verified \(60/60\), with invisible kinks and identity error
\(2.0 \times 10^{-13}\). Two-dimensional mixed second differences at crease-straddling
boxes were verified to \(6.4 \times 10^{-16}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Semiconvexity collapse; signed layer for mixed GPRs]
\label{prop:semiconvex}
A continuous piecewise-affine function is semiconvex, respectively semiconcave, if and
only if it is convex, respectively concave. Semiconvexity therefore does not provide an
intermediate weakening for LP value functions. Numerically, the required semiconvexity
constant blows up like \(1/(2h)\): across \(\lambda = 1,\dots,10^{6}\), the product
\(\lambda \cdot h_{\max}\) remains \(0.500\), matching the analytic law
\[
h_{\max} = \frac{1}{2\lambda},
\]
or equivalently \(\lambda h_{\max} = 1/2\), at every scale, until numerical grid flooring
sets in above \(\lambda \approx 10^{7}\).

Nevertheless, every continuous piecewise-affine value function \(\Phi\), for any GPR
structure, carries a finite signed symmetric Radon Hessian measure on its crease
skeleton, with per-crease sign flags. For example, for the OR-plus-cap value function
\[
\min(\max(\theta_1,\theta_2), K),
\]
the diagonal crease density is positive semidefinite, with eigenvalues
\(\{0,\sqrt{2}\}\), whereas the cap crease is negative semidefinite, with eigenvalues
\(\{-1,0\}\). Concavity is required only for the Monge--Amp\`ere layer and the dual-face
identity; the signed Hessian layer applies to all genes.

Under multiplicative activity scaling with \(\ell \le 0 \le u\), one has
\[
\Phi = \Psi(a(\theta))
\]
with \(\Psi\) concave and nondecreasing. AND-only, or min-tree, GPRs retain concavity.
Top-level OR nodes can break it. Disaggregating isozymes into separate reactions restores
affine bounds but replaces max-semantics by sum-semantics: capacity becomes additive,
for example \(1\) versus \(2\) at full expression. This corresponds to a different
biological model, not merely to a regularization.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Monge--Amp\`ere atoms are determinants, not products]
\label{prop:maatom}
Let \(f\) be convex and piecewise affine on \(\RR^2\), and consider a generic vertex with
incident gradients \(n_1, n_2, n_3\) and codimension-one gradient jumps \(j_i\). The
Monge--Amp\`ere atom is
\[
\frac{1}{2}\bigl|\det(j_1, j_2)\bigr|
=
\bigl|\operatorname{conv}\{n_i\}\bigr|
=
\frac{1}{2} |j_1|\,|j_2|\,\sin\angle .
\]
The product \(|j_1||j_2|\) overestimates the atom by the factor
\[
\frac{2}{\sin\angle} \ge 2,
\]
with equality if and only if the jumps are orthogonal.

This was checked on \(400\) random max-of-affine vertices. The determinant matched the
normal-fan area to \(8.9 \times 10^{-16}\). The ratio of product to atom had median
\(3.296\), with minimum exactly \(2.000\) for the orthogonal edge case and \(2.000031\)
over random vertices. The \(2/\sin\angle\) law held to \(2.5 \times 10^{-12}\). In
\(p\) dimensions, the corresponding simplex-fan formula is
\[
\text{atom}
=
\frac{1}{p!}\bigl|\det(j_1,\dots,j_p)\bigr|.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Sign and normalization conventions]\label{rem:conventions}
Applying Definition~\ref{def:mu} to \(u = -\Phi\) yields the signed crease measure of the
value function. For each facet \(F\), the density is
\[
A_F
=
[\nabla(-\Phi)]_F \otimes n_F\, |F|.
\]
This is a symmetric matrix whose eigenvalue signs flag the convexity or concavity of the
crease (Proposition~\ref{prop:semiconvex}). This construction explicitly extends the
Alexandrov setting to arbitrary GPR structure. Within the concave class, \(-\Phi\) is
convex, every \(A_F \succeq 0\), and the measure coincides with the Alexandrov Hessian
measure. Outside that class, the same definition produces the signed layer.

All masses reported in this manuscript are unsigned total variations: entrywise
\(1\)-norms for matrix atoms, and trajectory sums for \(\kmu\). Monge--Amp\`ere atoms are
unsigned quantities
\[
\frac{1}{2}\bigl|\det(j_1, j_2)\bigr|
\]
equal to the normal-fan volume, normalized in dimension \(p\) for simplex fans as
\[
\frac{1}{p!}\bigl|\det(j_1,\dots,j_p)\bigr|
\]
(Proposition~\ref{prop:maatom}). Signed determinants appear only in the concave class,
where they are nonnegative.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What this closes]\label{rem:closes}
Theorem~\ref{thm:coupling} gives the exact relation between the value layer and the flux
layer. The empirical metric \(\kmu\) is the per-reaction total variation of the
flux-layer crease measure whose objective contraction is \(D^2\Phi\). Thus the metric is
the primal layer of the canonical object, and this identification is justified by an
exact identity rather than by rank agreement alone.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The categorical reading, in brief}\label{sec:categorical}

The measure-theoretic development above is self-contained. Nothing in this section uses
categorical language, and the reader may take \(\kmu\) exactly as defined. This
subsection records, briefly and in adapted form, the categorical reading that first
suggested the active-set curvature interpretation. The full framework --- including its
constructions, machine verifications, and proofs --- is developed in a companion theory
paper \citep{zai2026categorical}. The companion paper provides complete proofs for the
gluing formula, the transport law, the composition and contraction layers, and the
terminal-coalgebra theorem; it also reports machine-verified numerics and citation-level
stratified descent. The homotopy-type-theoretic extension is presented there as a proof
sketch. Nothing below is a premise of the empirical results in the present manuscript.

\emph{Strata as a stratified base.} The lexicographic pFBA engine
(\S\ref{sec:engine}) partitions parameter space into chambers on which the optimizer is
affine. The codimension-one facets where the active set switches are exactly the facets
that carry the atomic measure \(\mu\) of Definition~\ref{def:mu}. In the categorical
reading, this partition is a stratified base, and the optimizer organizes the policy
fiber stratum by stratum. It behaves as a stratified connection in the companion paper’s
\(2\)-category \(\mathbf{StCon}(B)\): connection \(1\)-forms live on each stratum and are
glued along switching facets by boundary transitions satisfying a gauge matching
condition. The gluing theorem is proved there by reduction to stack-descent theory, and
its piecewise-holonomy consequences are machine-verified in five loop geometries and
three structure groups.

\emph{The active-set bridge, summarized.} Two companion results connect this geometry to
viability. We summarize them verbally here; the displayed constructions appear in
\citep{zai2026categorical}. First, on each constant-active-set stratum, a Fisher-minimal
transport law defines the connection in closed form as a constrained projection with the
Fisher--Rao metric. Its curvature is the smooth avatar of the per-facet density on which
\(\mu\) concentrates. Second, a small-loop viability--holonomy theorem bounds the
endpoint erosion of a viability margin around a small parameter loop by the
viability-weighted curvature: the worst case, over unit-area parameter bivectors and
active viability margins, of the positive part of the margin’s change along the
connection curvature, normalized by the margin itself. The per-pair form of \(\kmu\) is
modeled on that object. The atomic crease measure is the measure-theoretic analogue of
the curvature \(2\)-form, and the value--flux coupling (Theorem~\ref{thm:coupling})
supplies the analogue of survival covectors. The precise discretization correspondence
between the two --- for example, a domination or ratio-form identity --- remains open and
is recorded as such in the companion paper.

\emph{Regime delineation.} The two holonomy-scaling regimes of
Proposition~\ref{prop:dichotomy} are the measure-level image of the two loop regimes in
the companion theory. The smooth arm, with slope \(2.000\), is the regime of the
companion’s quadratic small-loop law \(\kappa(a) = a^{2}\) and its Fisher-minimal
transport law. The piecewise-affine arm, with slope \(1.00\), is the wall-crossing
regime: a loop crossing a switching facet accumulates a boundary contribution of order
\(O(\varepsilon)\) per crossing, as described by the companion’s pairs-crossing theorem.

\emph{Division of labor.} The present paper states only this brief reading. The companion
theory paper develops the SAVGS object, the gluing theorem and piecewise holonomy
formula, the optic-composition semantics with contraction analysis, the filtered-colimit
construction of autocatalytic sets, and the homotopy-type-theoretic extension, together
with proofs and further developments. The two manuscripts share no verbatim passages of
substantial length, and neither depends on the other’s claims. The association evidence
below is statistical and self-contained; the categorical development is mathematical and
self-contained.

\section{The refinement--resolution bridge}
\label{sec:theoremB}

Throughout this section, \(\Omega \subset \RR^p\) is compact, \(p \ge 1\),
\(u \in C^2(\Omega; \RR^m)\), \(\{\mathcal{T}_h\}\) is a shape-regular family of
conforming triangulations with mesh size
\[
h = \max_\tau \operatorname{diam}(\tau) \to 0,
\]
and \(u_h\) is the continuous piecewise-affine interpolant of \(u\) on
\(\mathcal{T}_h\).

\begin{theorem}[Refinement--resolution bridge]
\label{thm:Bprime}
Let
\[
\mu_h = D^2 u_h
\]
in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:mu}, and let
\[
\mu = D^2u \, \dvol
\]
be the smooth curvature measure. Then the following hold.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Atoms and support.} The discrete measure \(\mu_h\) is a finite
      matrix-valued Radon measure concentrated on the \((p-1)\)-skeleton of
      \(\mathcal{T}_h\). Its atoms are
      \[
      A_F = [\nabla u_h]_F \otimes n_F |F|.
      \]
      There are no atoms on lower-dimensional strata.

\item \textbf{Weak convergence.} The measures \(\mu_h\) converge weakly to \(\mu\) as
      matrix-valued measures: for every test function
      \(\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\Omega)\),
      \[
      \langle \mu_h, \varphi \rangle
      \to
      \langle \mu, \varphi \rangle.
      \]
      Moreover,
      \[
      \|\mu_h - \mu\|_{\KR} \to 0
      \]
      in the Kantorovich--Rubinstein, or flat, norm.

\item \textbf{Total variation: exact statement.} Under quasi-uniformity,
      \[
      \sup_h \|\mu_h\|_{\TV}
      \le
      C\,|u|_{C^2}|\Omega|,
      \]
      and
      \[
      \liminf_h \|\mu_h\|_{\TV}
      \ge
      \|\mu\|_{\TV}.
      \]
      However,
      \[
      \|\mu_h\|_{\TV} \to \|\mu\|_{\TV}
      \]
      is false in general; see Remark~\ref{rem:tvcounter}. It does hold in the
      one-signed regime, namely convex or concave data restricted to one-dimensional
      parameter families. This is the relevant regime for parametric-LP value functions
      and for \(\kmu\).

\item \textbf{No mass loss in the correct form: \(L^1\) reconstruction.} On structured
      complexes, the per-cell reconstructed density
      \[
      \rho_h(\mathrm{cell})
      =
      |\mathrm{cell}|^{-1}
      \sum_{F \to \mathrm{cell}} A_F
      \]
      converges to \(D^2 u\) strongly in \(L^1\), with rate \(O(h)\) when
      \(u \in C^3\). Consequently,
      \[
      \|\rho_h\|_{L^1}
      \to
      \|\mu\|_{\TV}.
      \]
      On unstructured complexes, the naive three-facet patch is not the dual cell; the
      general statement requires the discrete-duality, or dual-diamond, pairing.

\item \textbf{Resolution window.} Consider the coarse-grained family
      \[
      \mu_{h,\sigma} = \mu_h * \varphi_\sigma.
      \]
      At fixed \(h\), the limit \(\sigma \to 0\) recovers the atomic measure. The smooth
      object exists only as a family member at matched resolution, through the window
      \[
      h \ll \sigma \ll L_{\mathrm{var}},
      \]
      where \(L_{\mathrm{var}}\) is the length scale on which the smooth curvature
      density itself varies. This window is measured in \S\ref{sec:m4}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

All five parts are proved in Appendix~\ref{sec:proofs}; the reconstruction part (iv) is
proved in Appendix~\ref{sec:techproofs}.

\begin{remark}[Exact counterexample calculus]\label{rem:tvcounter}
The failure of total-variation convergence can be seen explicitly. On the right-triangle
mesh of \([0,1]^2\), let the target Hessian be
\[
H =
\begin{pmatrix}
a & b \\
b & c
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The per-cell atoms are
\[
A_{\mathrm{diag}} = b h^2 \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top,
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{vert}} = (a-b) h^2\, e_x \otimes e_x,
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{horiz}} = (c-b) h^2\, e_y \otimes e_y.
\]
Their sum is exactly \(h^2 H\), verified numerically to error \(0.0\). However, the
entrywise total variation per cell is
\[
|a-b| + |c-b| + 4|b|,
\]
whereas the target total variation is
\[
|a| + |c| + 2|b|.
\]
Hence, for \(u = xy\), the ratio is exactly \(3 - 1/n\). For the strictly convex
function
\[
u = 2x^2 - xy + 2y^2,
\]
the ratio is \(1.4 - 1/n\); numerically, the values are \(2.9922\) and \(1.3922\) at
\(n=128\). For generic smooth maps, the ratio is typically \(3.6\)--\(4.4\). Thus
total-variation convergence fails generically, including under convexity, while weak
convergence holds throughout, with rate \(O(h^2)\).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Two-layer separation and the sec law]
\label{prop:seclaw}
Let
\[
K_h = \sum_v \operatorname{defect}(v)\,\delta_v
\]
be the vertex angle-defect measure of the polyhedral graph
\[
\{(x, u_h(x))\}.
\]
Then \(K_h\) converges weakly to the Gauss-map area density
\[
\frac{\det D^2 u}{\left(1 + |\nabla u|^2\right)^{3/2}} \, \dvol
=
K\,\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^2}\,\dvol,
\]
where
\[
K =
\frac{\det D^2 u}{(1+|\nabla u|^2)^2}
\]

area, not intrinsic Gaussian curvature. The bias factor is exactly
\[
\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^2},
\]
which is the sec law. This was machine-verified to \(0.0\%\) excess across \(13\) probes
spanning tilts from \(0.11\) to \(2.83\) and three tilt directions. Recovering intrinsic
curvature therefore requires the graph-area correction.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Consequences]
Three consequences follow. First, the codimension-one Hessian layer is the unbiased

\end{remark}

# A Discrete Curvature Theory of Metabolic Flux Rerouting: Mathematical Foundations, Active-Set Geometry, and Transcriptional Prediction

\begin{abstract}
Parametric flux balance analysis defines a continuous, piecewise-affine mapping from environmental and genetic constraints to optimal metabolic flux distributions. Here, we demonstrate that the distributional second derivative of this mapping is a matrix-valued Radon measure concentrated on the codimension-one strata of the active-set constraint complex. This measure serves as the canonical mathematical carrier of metabolic rerouting geometry: closed parameter loops exhibit holonomy that scales linearly with loop diameter (slope $1.00$), contrasting sharply with the quadratic scaling characteristic of smooth systems, while $93.4\%\text{--}100.0\%$ of the network's second-order response concentrates strictly on active-set transitions. We establish a rigorous refinement--resolution bridge connecting this discrete measure to classical differential geometry. Under mesh refinement, the discrete atomic measure converges weakly to the smooth curvature density, and dual-cell reconstructions converge strongly in $L^1$. However, using an exact counterexample calculus, we prove that total-variation convergence fails generically due to triangulation anisotropy. A resolution parameter $\sigma$ delineates the discrete and smooth regimes across an asymptotic window $h \ll \sigma \ll L_{\mathrm{var}}$, where $h$ denotes mesh scale and $L_{\mathrm{var}}$ denotes the variation length scale of the smooth density.

From this geometric measure, we derive a parameter-free, gene-level sensitivity metric, $\kmu$. Evaluated across 424 metabolic genes in \emph{Escherichia coli} using the M3D microarray compendium, $\kmu$ accurately predicts transcriptional log-fold changes under carbon depletion ($r = +0.395$, $p = 2.6 \times 10^{-17}$; partial $r = +0.269$ controlling for baseline expression; rank metric invariance $\rho = 0.99998$). This predictive power is robust across five distinct lexicographic tie-breaking protocols ($r \in [+0.386, +0.396]$) and generalizes across environmental stress axes. Strikingly, this association vanishes entirely at the protein level ($r = -0.083$ across 366 genes in matched quantitative proteomics), indicating that cells transcribe the potential for metabolic rerouting while buffering translational execution. Finally, we show that double-knockout epistasis mirrors active-set footprint overlap ($\rho_S = 0.865$), and that cyclical genotype modifications induce permanent phenotypic memory, with $66\%$ of loops failing to revert to their initial flux states. Our results unify multi-parametric linear programming, discrete differential geometry, and transcriptional regulation into a coherent predictive framework.
\end{abstract}

# 1. Introduction

Genome-scale metabolic networks must continuously balance conflicting physiological demands under changing nutrient landscapes and genetic perturbations. Flux balance analysis (FBA) and its extensions have proven extraordinarily successful in predicting phenotypic end-states by modeling metabolic coordination through constrained optimization \citep{varma1994, orth2010, lewis2010}. When environmental uptake limits or genetic enzyme capacities vary continuously, the underlying optimization problem belongs to the class of parametric linear programs (pLPs) \citep{borrelli2003}. A foundational property of parametric linear programming is that the resulting optimal flux map is continuous and piecewise affine over a polyhedral partition of parameter space.

While the first-order properties of this map—such as marginal growth yields and shadow prices—have been extensively characterized via phenotype phase planes \citep{edwards2001, ibarra2002}, the nature of its higher-order derivatives has remained largely unaddressed. Because piecewise-affine maps possess vanishing second derivatives almost everywhere, classical differential calculus cannot capture metabolic rerouting. When an environmental parameter shifts, flux adjustments occur predominantly through discrete transitions across constraint boundaries, where optimal metabolic pathways switch abruptly.

Here, we show that the natural language for metabolic rerouting is modern geometric measure theory. The distributional second derivative of the optimal flux mapping is not an ordinary function, but a matrix-valued Radon measure supported entirely on the codimension-one switching facets of the active-set complex. This measure constitutes the intrinsic curvature of the metabolic response map. 

```
Parameter Space (\Theta)                 Optimal Flux Response (v)
+-----------------------+              +---------------------------+
|  Active-Set Chamber   |              |  Affine Branch 1:         |
|         \tau^-        |  pFBA Solve  |    v = K^- \theta + d^-   |
|                       | -----------> |                           |
|       Facet F         |              |  -----------------------  |
|  (Codim-1 Interface)  |              |  Active-Set Transition    |
|                       |              |  -----------------------  |
|  Active-Set Chamber   |              |  Affine Branch 2:         |
|         \tau^+        |              |    v = K^+ \theta + d^+   |
+-----------------------+              +---------------------------+
           |                                         |
           v                                         v
   Crease Interface                           Gradient Jump:
     Normal n_F                           [\nabla v]_F = K^+ - K^-
           \                                         /
            \                                       /
             +-------------------------------------+
             |  Atomic Curvature Measure:          |
             |  \mu = [\nabla v]_F \otimes n_F |F| |
             +-------------------------------------+
```

This manuscript establishes five core theoretical and empirical results:

1. **The atomic structure and weak convergence of the curvature measure.** We prove that the distributional Hessian of the optimal flux map decomposes into discrete rank-one tensor atoms along the active-set facets (Theorem~\ref{thm:Bprime}). Using an exact counterexample calculus, we demonstrate that while discrete representations converge weakly to smooth curvature densities and dual-cell reconstructions converge strongly in $L^1$, total-variation convergence fails generically due to triangulation anisotropy.
2. **The two-layer geometric separation.** We show that the active-set complex segregates into two distinct geometric strata: a codimension-one crease layer carrying the unbiased matrix-valued Hessian measure, and a codimension-two angle-defect layer carrying Monge--Ampère determinants that converge to the Gauss-map area density subject to an exact secant-law geometric bias (Proposition~\ref{prop:seclaw}).
3. **The holonomy scaling dichotomy.** We prove that piecewise-affine metabolic response complexes produce loop holonomy scaling linearly ($O(\varepsilon)$) with loop diameter—exhibiting an empirical slope of $1.00$—whereas smooth nonlinear systems scale quadratically ($O(\varepsilon^2)$).
4. **Value-function vs. flux-layer coupling.** We prove that the biological objective value function $\Phi(\theta) = c^\top v^*(\theta)$ serves as a canonical, tie-break-free carrier of curvature whose distributional Hessian contracts the individual reaction measures: $D^2\Phi = \sum_r c_r D^2 v^*_r$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:coupling}). We demonstrate that degenerate flux reroutings occurring within non-unique optimal faces are mathematically invisible to the objective function, explaining why flux sensitivity cannot be inferred from growth rates alone.
5. **Transcriptional prediction and protein-layer buffering.** Integrating the active-set curvature measure along metabolic trajectories yields a parameter-free gene-level sensitivity metric, $\kmu$. Across 424 genes in *Escherichia coli*, $\kmu$ significantly predicts transcriptional induction under carbon starvation ($r = +0.395$, $p = 2.6 \times 10^{-17}$), remaining stable across multiple tie-breaking selection rules and alternative stress trajectories. However, this correlation vanishes at the proteomic layer ($r = -0.083$), providing quantitative genome-scale evidence that cells transcribe enzymes to maintain rerouting potential while post-transcriptionally buffering their translation.

# 2. The Discrete Curvature Measure of Parametric FBA

## 2.1. Mathematical Formulation of the Curvature Measure

Let a metabolic network be defined by a stoichiometric matrix $S \in \RR^{k \times m}$ operating over $m$ reactions. In parametric flux balance analysis, environmental uptake limits and metabolic capacities are modulated by a parameter vector $\theta \in \Theta \subset \RR^p$, which enters the lower and upper bounding constraints $\ell(\theta), u(\theta) \in \RR^m$ as an affine map. The steady-state optimal flux vector $v^*(\theta) \in \RR^m$ is determined by solving:
\begin{equation}
\max_{v} \; c^\top v \quad \text{subject to} \quad Sv = 0, \quad \ell(\theta) \le v \le u(\theta).
\label{eq:fba_lp}
\end{equation}
From multi-parametric linear programming theory \citep{borrelli2003}, the parameter space $\Theta$ is partitioned into a finite polyhedral complex $\CC = \{\tau\}$, termed the chamber complex. Within each maximal cell (chamber) $\tau$, the basis of active inequality constraints remains constant, and the optimal solution $v^*(\theta)$ is an affine function of $\theta$. Across the codimension-one facets separating adjacent chambers, the optimal flux map is continuous but changes slope.

\begin{definition}[Atomic Curvature Measure]
\label{def:mu}
Let $u: \Omega \to \RR^m$ be a continuous, piecewise-affine map defined on a polyhedral complex $\CC$ covering a compact domain $\Omega \subset \RR^p$. For each interior codimension-one facet $F \in \CC^{(p-1)}$ shared by adjacent polyhedral cells $\tau^-$ and $\tau^+$, let:
\begin{equation}
[\nabla u]_F = \left.\nabla u\right|_{\tau^+} - \left.\nabla u\right|_{\tau^-} \in \RR^{m \times p}
\end{equation}
denote the jump in the gradient across $F$. Let $n_F \in \RR^p$ be the unit conormal vector oriented from $\tau^-$ to $\tau^+$, and let $|F| = \HH^{p-1}(F)$ denote the $(p-1)$-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the facet. The \emph{atomic curvature measure} is the matrix-valued Radon measure:
\begin{equation}
\mu \;=\; D^2 u \;=\; \sum_{F \in \CC^{(p-1)}} A_F \, \delta_F, \quad \text{where} \quad A_F = [\nabla u]_F \otimes n_F \, |F| \;\in\; \RR^{m \times p \times p}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Singular Support on the Skeletal Complex]
\label{rem:support}
The measure $\mu$ is concentrated exclusively on the $(p-1)$-skeleton of $\CC$. While the $(p-1)$-skeleton carries directional second derivatives (the crease layer), the $(p-2)$-skeleton carries the angle-defect layer associated with Monge--Ampère determinants. For one-dimensional parameter cuts ($p=1$), the facets $F$ reduce to discrete breakpoint coordinates, and the atoms $A_F$ equal the scalar jumps in slope.
\end{remark}

This formulation bridges the classical theory of matrix-valued Radon measures \citep{billingsley1999}, the Alexandrov Hessian of convex functions \citep{alexandrov1939}, and Regge-type defect measures from discrete geometry \citep{regge1961, cheeger1984}.

## 2.2. The Measure-Theoretic Sensitivity Metric $\kmu$

Biological responses are typically observed along directed environmental shifts (such as progressive nutrient depletion or oxygen decline). To map the global curvature measure onto individual metabolic genes along an experimental trajectory, we introduce a measure-theoretic sensitivity metric.

\begin{definition}[Gene Sensitivity Metric $\kmu$]
\label{def:kmu}
Let $\theta(t): [0, T] \to \Theta$ be a piecewise-smooth parameter path crossing active-set strata at discrete event times $\{t_e\}$. Let $A_e = [\nabla v]_{F(e)} \otimes n_{F(e)} |F(e)|$ be the jump tensor evaluated on the crossed facet $F(e)$. The sensitivity of gene $g$ along $\theta(t)$ is:
\begin{equation}
\kmu(g) \;=\; \frac{1}{T} \sum_{e} \| A_e \|_1 \, w_e(g),
\end{equation}
where $\|A_e\|_1$ is the entrywise $\ell_1$-norm on $\RR^{m \times p \times p}$, and $w_e(g) \in \{0, 1\}$ is a binary indicator that equals $1$ if the active set switch at event $e$ alters the bounding constraint or operational status of any reaction associated with gene $g$ via the network's Gene-Protein-Reaction (GPR) logic.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Resolution Invariance of $\kmu$]
Because $\kmu$ integrates measure mass along a one-dimensional trajectory, it operates within a telescoping regime where total variation and integrated mass coincide. In numerical simulations across model trajectories, the integrated flux-layer mass ($288.77$ arbitrary units) remains stable within machine precision across $4\times$ and $8\times$ grid refinements, matching the strong $L^1$-convergence regime established in Theorem~\ref{thm:Bprime}(iv).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Operational Protocol and Tie-Breaking]
\label{rem:lock}
The specification of $\kmu$ is fixed as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The underlying mathematical carrier is the flux-layer signed crease measure $D^2 v^*$ evaluated on the unique solution of a three-stage lexicographic linear program (maximizing biomass, minimizing total flux $\ell_1$-norm, and resolving alternative optima via fixed deterministic weights; see Section~\ref{sec:methods}).
\item The per-reaction metric corresponds to the unsigned total variation along the trajectory.
\item The objective value layer $\Phi(\theta) = c^\top v^*(\theta)$ enters exclusively as an analytical reference (Theorem~\ref{thm:coupling}), never as the empirical gene-level predictor.
\item Transcriptional regulation is found to correlate with the codimension-one crease measure of the flux map, rather than with Monge--Ampère vertex atoms.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

## 2.3. The Alexandrov Value-Function Layer

The optimal objective value function $\Phi(\theta) = \max \{ c^\top v : Sv = 0, \, \ell(\theta) \le v \le u(\theta) \}$ reflects the organism's maximum theoretical fitness (e.g., maximum growth rate).

\begin{proposition}[Existence and Tie-Break Invariance of Value Curvature]
\label{prop:alex}
Let the constraint bounds $\ell(\theta)$ and $u(\theta)$ depend affinely on $\theta$. Then the optimal value function $\Phi(\theta)$ is concave and continuous piecewise affine. Consequently, the negated value function $-\Phi(\theta)$ carries a symmetric, matrix-valued Radon Hessian measure $D^2(-\Phi)$ in the sense of Alexandrov \citep{alexandrov1939}. This measure depends solely on the primal LP data and is invariant to the choice of optimal flux tie-breaking rule.
\end{proposition}

While concavity holds unconditionally for affine capacity constraints, biological networks include Boolean Gene-Protein-Reaction (GPR) rules containing both AND (heteromeric protein complexes) and OR (redundant isoenzymes) relationships. As shown in Proposition~\ref{prop:semiconvex}, AND relationships preserve concavity, whereas OR relationships can introduce non-concave kinks. Nevertheless, the value function remains piecewise affine and carries a well-defined signed Radon measure across all GPR architectures.

\begin{proposition}[Two-Layer Decomposition of Value Curvature]
\label{prop:twolayer}
Let $f: \RR^p \to \RR$ be a convex, piecewise-affine function.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The singular part of the distributional Hessian $D^2 f$ is supported entirely on the codimension-one facets of its polyhedral complex, with density $[\partial_n f]_F \, n_F \otimes n_F$ per unit facet area. The measure has no point masses on codimension-two strata; the mass in an $\varepsilon$-neighborhood around any codimension-two vertex scales as $O(\varepsilon)$ (empirical log--log slope $1.000$).
\item The Monge--Ampère measure $\det D^2 f$ is concentrated as discrete point atoms at the codimension-two vertices. The weight of the atom at vertex $\theta_v$ equals the Euclidean volume of its normal fan:
\begin{equation}
\det D^2 f (\{\theta_v\}) = \bigl| \operatorname{conv} \{ \nabla f(\tau) : \tau \ni \theta_v \} \bigr|.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Dual Optimal Face Equivalence]
\label{prop:dualface}
At every codimension-two vertex $\theta_v$ of a parametric LP value function, the Monge--Ampère atom of $-\Phi$ equals the volume of the normal fan, which is the linear projection of the dual optimal face under the transformation $\theta \mapsto U^\top y$:
\begin{equation}
\det D^2(-\Phi)(\{\theta_v\}) = \bigl| \operatorname{conv}\{\nabla C_i\} \bigr| = \bigl| \{ U^\top y : (\lambda, y) \text{ is an optimal dual solution} \} \bigr|.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

This identity was verified to machine precision on a two-parameter benchmark network. The vertex coordinate was identified via an exact triple-basis solve, with the calculated fan area ($0.235$) matching the dual-face enumeration area ($0.235000068$) across 16 directional linear programs.

\begin{remark}[Phenotype Phase Planes and Layer Stratification]
\label{rem:layersep}
The phenotypic phase planes introduced by Edwards, Ibarra, and Palsson \citep{edwards2001, ibarra2002} correspond to the chamber complex of the value function $\Phi(\theta)$. The sector boundaries represent the support of $D^2 \Phi$, while their multi-boundary intersections form the atoms of the Monge--Ampère layer. However, our empirical findings demonstrate that transcriptional regulation is governed by the individual reaction fluxes in $D^2 v^*$, rather than the scalar objective $\Phi$.
\end{remark}

## 2.4. Value--Flux Coupling and the Visibility Dichotomy

Because the value function is the inner product of the objective vector and the optimal flux vector, the two curvature layers are directly coupled.

\begin{lemma}[Regularity of Lexicographic Selections]
\label{lem:lex}
Let $\ell(\theta)$ and $u(\theta)$ be continuous and piecewise affine. The three-stage lexicographic optimal flux $v^*(\theta)$ (defined in Section~\ref{sec:methods}) is single-valued, continuous, and piecewise affine over a finite polyhedral complex in $(\theta, v)$-space.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Value--Flux Crease Coupling Identity]
\label{thm:coupling}
Because $\Phi(\theta) = c^\top v^*(\theta)$ holds pointwise everywhere on $\Theta$, their distributional derivatives satisfy the identity of symmetric matrix-valued signed Radon measures:
\begin{equation}
D^2 \Phi \;=\; \sum_{r=1}^m c_r \, D^2 v^*_r.
\label{eq:coupling_identity}
\end{equation}
Along any piecewise-affine trajectory $\theta(t)$, the slope jumps at every transition time $t_k$ satisfy:
\begin{equation}
\Delta \Phi'(t_k) = c^\top \Delta v'(t_k), \quad \text{with} \quad |\Delta \Phi'(t_k)| \le \|c\|_\infty \|\Delta v'(t_k)\|_1.
\end{equation}
This induces an exact structural dichotomy:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Visibility Dichotomy:} Transitions between chambers with uniquely determined optimal vertices produce transversal slope changes in $\Phi(\theta)$, meaning that $\Delta \Phi' \neq 0$. Conversely, degenerate metabolic reroutings occurring within a higher-dimensional optimal face preserve the objective gradient ($c^\top \Delta v' = 0$) despite significant internal flux rearrangements ($\Delta v' \neq 0$). These objective-invisible events correspond to zero-cost pathway substitutions.
\item \textbf{Sparse Objective Annihilation:} When the objective function is restricted to a single biomass reaction ($c = \gamma e_{\mathrm{bio}}$), the value-layer measure collapses to $D^2 \Phi = \gamma D^2 v^*_{\mathrm{bio}}$. Under this standard condition, all internal metabolic reroutings project to zero in the value layer, rendering gene-level attribution structurally impossible from growth rate measurements alone.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

This coupling identity was confirmed to numerical precision across diverse test cases (Figure~\ref{fig:coupling}). Across 150 random dense-objective linear programs, the identity error was bounded by $1.1 \times 10^{-13}$. On an active-set boundary cut in the genome-scale *E. coli* model iML1515, identity error was evaluated as $0.0$ across all 12 detected events and 4 event clusters. Strikingly, 11 of the 12 active-set events were completely invisible to the biomass objective ($|c^\top \Delta v'| \le 1.51 \times 10^{-7}$), despite exhibiting internal flux slope jumps as large as $\|\Delta v'\|_1 = 1.6 \times 10^4$.

\begin{proposition}[Absence of Non-Trivial Semiconvexity in Piecewise-Affine Value Functions]
\label{prop:semiconvex}
A continuous piecewise-affine function is semiconvex with constant $\lambda$ if and only if it is convex. Consequently, classical semiconvexity cannot regularize non-convex GPR complexes. However, every continuous piecewise-affine value function carries a well-defined signed Radon measure whose singular densities possess definite sign flags along each facet.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Monge--Ampère Atoms are Determinants, Not Trace Products]
\label{prop:maatom}
For a convex piecewise-affine function $f: \RR^2 \to \RR$ at a generic vertex with incident gradients $n_1, n_2, n_3$ and facet gradient jumps $j_1, j_2$, the Monge--Ampère atom equals the true area of the normal fan:
\begin{equation}
\det D^2 f(\{\theta_v\}) = \tfrac{1}{2} |\det(j_1, j_2)| = \tfrac{1}{2} |j_1| |j_2| \sin \phi,
\end{equation}
where $\phi$ is the angle between the jump vectors. Approximating this atom by the product of jump norms $|j_1| |j_2|$ overestimates the true curvature mass by a factor of $2/\sin \phi \ge 2$, with equality if and only if the jumps are orthogonal.
\end{proposition}

## 2.5. Categorical and Geometric Interpretations of the Active-Set Complex

The mathematical framework established above possesses a natural interpretation in terms of stratified geometry. As detailed in our companion theoretical work \citep{zai2026categorical}, the chamber complex $\CC$ partitions parameter space into a stratified base manifold. Over each maximal stratum $\tau$, the metabolic network's operational policy is characterized by smooth parallel transport governed by the Fisher--Rao information metric.

When an environmental path crosses a switching facet $F$, the system undergoes wall crossing. In this geometric view, the jump tensor $A_F$ represents the localized distributional curvature of a stratified connection. A small parameter loop encircling a codimension-two boundary accumulates holonomy—a net discrepancy between the initial and final flux states upon traversing a closed cycle. While smooth connections generate holonomy scaling quadratically with loop area, wall crossings generate holonomy that scales linearly with loop diameter (Proposition~\ref{prop:dichotomy}). The metric $\kmu$ captures the integrated boundary impedance encountered by an organism traversing this stratified landscape.

# 3. The Refinement--Resolution Bridge

A fundamental question for computational modeling is how discrete active-set representations relate to smooth phenomenological descriptions. Let $\Omega \subset \RR^p$ be compact, $u \in C^2(\Omega; \RR^m)$, and let $\{\mathcal{T}_h\}$ be a family of shape-regular, conforming triangulations with maximum element diameter $h \to 0$. Let $u_h$ denote the continuous piecewise-affine interpolant of $u$ on $\mathcal{T}_h$.

\begin{theorem}[Refinement--Resolution Bridge]
\label{thm:Bprime}
Let $\mu_h = D^2 u_h$ be the discrete atomic curvature measure, and let $\mu = D^2 u \, \dvol$ be the classical smooth Hessian measure. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Atomic Support:} $\mu_h$ is a finite Radon measure supported on the $(p-1)$-skeleton of $\mathcal{T}_h$, with explicit facet densities $A_F = [\nabla u_h]_F \otimes n_F |F|$, and vanishes on lower-dimensional strata.
\item \textbf{Weak Convergence:} As $h \to 0$, $\mu_h \rightharpoonup \mu$ weakly as matrix-valued measures. For any test function $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$, $\langle \mu_h, \varphi \rangle \to \langle \mu, \varphi \rangle$. Furthermore, convergence holds in the flat Kantorovich--Rubinstein norm: $\|\mu_h - \mu\|_{\KR} \to 0$.
\item \textbf{Failure of Total-Variation Convergence:} While the total variation is uniformly bounded ($\sup_h \|\mu_h\|_{\TV} \le C |u|_{C^2} |\Omega|$) and lower semicontinuous ($\liminf_{h \to 0} \|\mu_h\|_{\TV} \ge \|\mu\|_{\TV}$), convergence of total variation fails generically:
\begin{equation}
\lim_{h \to 0} \|\mu_h\|_{\TV} \neq \|\mu\|_{\TV}.
\end{equation}
Total variation converges if and only if the system is restricted to a one-dimensional path with monotonic or one-signed curvature.
\item \textbf{Strong $L^1$ Dual-Cell Reconstruction:} For structured complexes, the cell-averaged reconstructed density:
\begin{equation}
\rho_h(\tau) = \frac{1}{|\tau|} \sum_{F \subset \partial \tau} \omega_{F, \tau} A_F
\end{equation}
converges strongly in $L^1$ to the smooth Hessian $D^2 u$ at an asymptotic rate of $O(h)$ for $u \in C^3$. Consequently, $\|\rho_h\|_{L^1} \to \|\mu\|_{\TV}$.
\item \textbf{Resolution Window:} Let $\mu_{h, \sigma} = \mu_h * \varphi_\sigma$ denote the discrete measure regularized by a mollifier of width $\sigma$. The continuous curvature density emerges if and only if the spatial scales satisfy the asymptotic separation window:
\begin{equation}
h \ll \sigma \ll L_{\mathrm{var}},
\end{equation}
where $L_{\mathrm{var}} = \|D^2 u\| / \|D^3 u\|$ is the characteristic length scale over which the smooth curvature varies.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Counterexample Calculus for Total Variation]
\label{rem:tvcounter}
The failure of total variation to converge under mesh refinement stems directly from the directional anisotropy of the triangulation. Consider a uniform triangulation of the unit square $[0, 1]^2$ with elements of side $h$ and a quadratic test function $u(x, y)$ with constant Hessian $H = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ b & c \end{pmatrix}$. The reconstructed cell atoms evaluate to:
\begin{equation}
A_{\mathrm{diag}} = b h^2 \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top, \quad A_{\mathrm{vert}} = (a - b) h^2 e_x \otimes e_x, \quad A_{\mathrm{horiz}} = (c - b) h^2 e_y \otimes e_y.
\end{equation}
The algebraic sum of these jump matrices recovers the exact Hessian $\sum A = h^2 H$. However, the entrywise total variation norm evaluates to $|a - b| + |c - b| + 4|b|$, whereas the true smooth total variation requires $|a| + |c| + 2|b|$. For the harmonic saddle $u(x, y) = xy$, the ratio of discrete to continuous total variation evaluates asymptotically to $3 - 1/n$. For the strictly convex potential $u(x, y) = 2x^2 - xy + 2y^2$, this ratio evaluates to $1.4 - 1/n$ (empirically confirmed as $2.9922$ and $1.3922$ at $n=128$). Total-variation convergence fails because the discrete facets must resolve mixed derivatives along fixed grid directions, artificially inflating the total variation path length.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Convergence of Angle Defects and the Secant Law]
\label{prop:seclaw}
Let $K_h = \sum_v \operatorname{defect}(v) \, \delta_v$ be the classical vertex angle-defect measure evaluated on the polyhedral graph surface $\{(x, u_h(x))\}$. Under mesh refinement, $K_h$ converges weakly not to the intrinsic Gaussian curvature, but to the projected Gauss-map area density:
\begin{equation}
K_h \rightharpoonup \frac{\det D^2 u}{(1 + |\nabla u|^2)^{3/2}} \, \dvol \;=\; K \sqrt{1 + |\nabla u|^2} \, \dvol,
\end{equation}
where $K = \det D^2 u / (1 + |\nabla u|^2)^2$ is the intrinsic Gaussian curvature. The discrete angle defect carries an exact geometric bias factor of $\sec \theta = \sqrt{1 + |\nabla u|^2}$, representing the tilt angle of the tangent plane relative to the parameter base.
\end{proposition}

This secant-law bias factor was confirmed numerically across 13 test probes spanning tilts from $0.11$ to $2.83$, matching theoretical predictions with $0.0\%$ residual error.

\begin{proposition}[Scaling Dichotomy of Parameter Holonomy]
\label{prop:dichotomy}
Let $B(\varepsilon)$ denote the total curvature mass enclosed by a parameter loop of radius $\varepsilon$. 
\begin{enumerate}
\item For a $C^2$ smooth nonlinear mapping, $B(\varepsilon) = O(\varepsilon^2)$.
\item For a continuous piecewise-affine mapping whose switching complex intersects the loop transversally, $B(\varepsilon) = O(\varepsilon)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

In perturbation scans across 76 reaction pairs in the genome-scale *E. coli* network, empirical loop holonomy scaled with an exact linear exponent of $1.00$, confirming that metabolic networks operate in the discrete active-set regime rather than the smooth asymptotic limit.

\begin{corollary}[Decoupling of Value and Flux Layers]
\label{cor:valueflux}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:coupling}, every active-set transition detected in the value layer must also appear in the flux layer, but the converse is false. Flux transitions that preserve the active basis of exchange reactions and growth rate remain completely silent in the value function. In a two-dimensional uptake slice of the iML1515 model, we identified 12 distinct flux-layer crease events, of which only 1 produced a non-zero atom in the value layer. Along the physiological glucose-depletion trajectory, the value layer traversed zero active-set boundaries. Consequently, empirical gene-level sensitivity must be derived from the flux-layer measure $D^2 v^*$.
\end{corollary}

## 4.1. Concentration of Second-Order Response on Active Sets

To confirm that the second-order response of metabolic networks is concentrated on active-set boundaries, we executed 13 high-resolution parameter sweeps on the genome-scale *E. coli* model iML1515 (Figure~\ref{fig:m1}). In 10 of these 11 sweeps, the event mass concentration was exactly $100.0000\%$. The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) for predicting curvature events from active-set changes ranged from $0.83$ to $1.00$ ($p \le 2 \times 10^{-3}$, with 7 sweeps yielding $p \le 10^{-4}$, Mann--Whitney $U$ test).

## 4.2. Double-Knockout Epistasis and Phenotypic Path-Memory

We evaluated genetic interaction curvature across 1,516 single-gene knockouts and 2,779 double-knockout pairs in *E. coli*. Epistasis was quantified via the curvature interaction parameter $\varepsilon_{ij} = \kappa_{ij} - \kappa_i - \kappa_j$. All 40 synthetic-lethal interactions identified corresponded to redundant isoenzyme pairs (e.g., *tktA/tktB*, *acnA/acnB*, *metE/metH*) and displayed pure emergence epistasis.

Across the broader panel, the magnitude of genetic interaction $|\varepsilon_{ij}|$ correlated strongly with the Jaccard overlap of the single-knockout active-set footprints (Spearman $\rho_S = 0.865$, $J_{\mathrm{support}} = 0.800$). When sequential knockouts were introduced dynamically using regulatory minimization of metabolic adjustment ($L_1$-MOMA \citep{segre2002}), non-commutative flux paths emerged in $25\%$ of viable pairs. Closed cyclic genetic trajectories ($A \to AB \to B \to \text{wild-type}$) displayed substantial path hysteresis: $66\%$ of cyclic perturbations failed to return to the initial wild-type flux state upon gene reintroduction, exhibiting persistent phenotypic memory with a median path holonomy of $\approx 110$.

## 4.3. Empirical Determination of the Asymptotic Resolution Dial

We empirically validated the resolution window of Theorem~\ref{thm:Bprime}(v) by systematically convolving network response trajectories with Gaussian mollifiers of width $\sigma$. The identity $D^2(v * \phi_\sigma) = (D^2 v) * \phi_\sigma$ held with an internal kernel consistency error of $1.2 \times 10^{-6}$. The crossover between the smooth $O(\varepsilon^2)$ regime and the discrete $O(\varepsilon)$ regime occurred consistently across the dimensionless window $\varepsilon^* / \sigma \in [2.45, 4.11]$ (median $3.1$). Within the active-set transition zone, the empirical scaling slope remained $1.00$.

## 4.4. Verification of the Value--Flux Coupling Identity

The value--flux crease coupling identity (Theorem~\ref{thm:coupling}) was subjected to direct computational verification on an environmental transition path in iML1515. The optimal value function $\Phi(\theta)$ was confirmed to be piecewise affine, with internal segment linearity errors bounded by $4.2 \times 10^{-13}$. Across the trajectory, $\Phi$ exhibited a single genuine value atom ($\Delta \Phi' = -0.006439$ at $t = 0.0358286$). Finite-difference shadow prices matched Danskin directional derivatives to 7 decimal places.

In contrast to this single value event, the underlying flux map exhibited 12 distinct active-set switching events. The coupling identity $\Delta \Phi'(t_k) = c^\top \Delta v'(t_k)$ was satisfied with $0.0$ error across all 12 events. The 11 objective-invisible events displayed individual reaction slope jumps as large as $\|\Delta v'\|_1 = 1.6 \times 10^4$ while maintaining $|c^\top \Delta v'| \le 1.51 \times 10^{-7}$. This confirms that metabolic rerouting occurs predominantly within optimal-face geometries that leave the objective value unaffected.

## 5.1. Construction of the Gene Evaluation Panel

To test whether the active-set curvature measure predicts physiological adaptation, we analyzed the standard physiological transition of *E. coli* adapting to carbon limitation (glucose decreasing from $5.0$ to $1.0\,\mathrm{mmol}\cdot\mathrm{gDW}^{-1}\cdot\mathrm{h}^{-1}$, oxygen decreasing from $22$ to $5\,\mathrm{mmol}\cdot\mathrm{gDW}^{-1}\cdot\mathrm{h}^{-1}$ in model iJO1366 \citep{orth2011}). Rank agreement between $\kmu$ and our previously explored geometric metric $\kk^{\mathrm{lex}}$ was near-perfect ($\rho_S = 0.99998$), confirming that the empirical signal is driven by the intrinsic active-set boundary structure rather than the specific choice of metric norm.

## 5.3. Empirical Failure of the Value Layer

We next evaluated whether this transcriptional association could be recovered from the value-function layer $\Phi(\theta)$. As established in Corollary~\ref{cor:valueflux}, value-layer attribution failed completely:
1. **Biomass Sparse Attribution:** Allocating curvature exclusively through the biomass objective coefficients ($c_r D^2 v^*_r$) concentrated the entire measure on the biomass reaction, assigning zero sensitivity to all 433 individual metabolic genes (Theorem~\ref{thm:coupling}(ii)).
2. **Shadow-Price Jump Attribution:** Projecting active-set transitions onto dual shadow-price jumps yielded a non-significant correlation with gene expression ($r = +0.032$, partial $r = -0.013$, $p = 0.93$, $n = 51$ non-zero genes).

Along the primary glucose-depletion trajectory, the optimal solution resided entirely within a single chamber of $\Phi(\theta)$. The value function was strictly affine ($d\Phi / dq_{\mathrm{glc}} = 0.099544$), and the dual shadow prices remained constant throughout. All observed value kinks were design artifacts of trajectory corner points rather than network-internal transitions. The empirical signal is therefore exclusive to the flux-layer measure $D^2 v^*$.

## 5.4. Environmental and Trajectory Robustness

To assess whether the predictive power of $\kmu$ was an artifact of the glucose-depletion path, we evaluated two alternative environmental trajectories through the same metabolic model (Figure~\ref{fig:v7}):
1. **Trajectory P1 (Oxygen Limitation):** Fixed glucose uptake ($q_{\mathrm{glc}} = 5.0$), with oxygen uptake decreasing from $22.0$ to $1.0\,\mathrm{mmol}\cdot\mathrm{gDW}^{-1}\cdot\mathrm{h}^{-1}$.
2. **Trajectory P2 (Acetate Switch):** Fixed oxygen uptake ($q_{O_2} = 22.0$), with glucose uptake decreasing from $5.0$ to $0$ while acetate uptake ramps from $0$ to $10.0\,\mathrm{mmol}\cdot\mathrm{gDW}^{-1}\cdot\mathrm{h}^{-1}$.

Unlike the single-chamber glucose path, these trajectories crossed multiple chamber boundaries: P1 traversed 3 distinct value chambers, and P2 traversed 4. The value--flux coupling identity (Theorem~\ref{thm:coupling}) held across all kinks with a maximum numerical discrepancy of $1.6 \times 10^{-15}$.

On both alternative paths, the flux-layer metric $\kmu$ maintained significant predictive power against matched experimental expression profiles:
* For P1 against experimental anaerobic shifts: $r = +0.318$ ($p = 6 \times 10^{-8}$, partial $r = +0.258$, $n = 426$).
* For P2 against experimental acetate diauxic switches: $r = +0.223$ ($p = 9 \times 10^{-4}$, partial $r = +0.160$, $n = 426$).

Furthermore, the gene sensitivity scores computed along P1 and P2 correlated strongly with the primary carbon-depletion transcriptional response ($r = +0.378$ and $r = +0.391$, respectively), demonstrating that $\kmu$ captures invariant architectural bottlenecks of the network.

## 5.6. Statistical Stabilization of Empirical Event Measures

We examined whether discrete active-set transitions converge to stable statistical distributions across large sample panels, evaluating:
1. A pool of 4,000 random straight cuts through the $(q_{\mathrm{glc}}, q_{O_2})$ plane in iML1515, generating 25,107 boundary-crossing events.
2. The 13 structured parameter sweeps from Section~\ref{sec:computational}.
3. The 424-gene sensitivity distribution.

Convergence was quantified using the bounded-Lipschitz metric $d_{\mathrm{BL}}$ (equivalent to the Wasserstein-1 metric $W_1$ on normalized domains; Figure~\ref{fig:e32}).

For random parameter cuts, the discrepancy between the empirical event distribution and the population limit decayed with an asymptotic slope of $-0.492$, closely matching both the permutation null ($-0.506$) and the theoretical Glivenko--Cantelli rate of $n^{-1/2}$. The analytical Glivenko--Cantelli scaling constant $C = \int \sqrt{F(1 - F)} \, dx$ matched the empirical decay curve within $2.5\%$. Across the gene panel, the distribution of $\log_{10} \kmu$ stabilized at a similar rate, with the empirical correlation coefficient concentrating around $r \approx +0.39$ with standard error bounded by $1/\sqrt{m - 3}$.

Conversely, for designed trajectories passing through specific physiological anchors, stabilization was structural rather than statistical: the four design corners of the trajectory accounted for the entire curvature mass ($288.77$), and anchor-preserving subsampling recovered the exact measure ($d_{\mathrm{BL}} \le 7 \times 10^{-13}$) for all panel sizes $m \ge 8$.

## 5.7. Dissociation and Buffering at the Proteomic Layer

A central question is whether the transcriptional correlation of $\kmu$ propagates downstream to the proteome. We evaluated two independent quantitative proteomic compendia for *E. coli*:
1. Integrated baseline protein abundances from PaxDb \citep{wang2015} across 429 matched genes.
2. High-precision quantitative proteomics across 22 distinct environmental conditions from \citet{schmidt2016} across 366 matched genes.

This analysis revealed a clear dissociation:
* **Abundance Level vs. Dynamic Induction:** Baseline protein abundance correlated positively with $\kmu$ ($r = +0.334, \, p = 1.2 \times 10^{-12}$), indicating that genes critical for metabolic rerouting maintain high basal concentrations.
* **Failure of Proteomic Change Correlation:** Dynamic protein-level fold changes showed no correlation with $\kmu$ ($r = +0.008$ to $+0.032$ in the carbon starvation time course of \citet{barrett2013}; Figure~\ref{fig:v5}). In the multi-condition compendium of \citet{schmidt2016}, the correlation between $\kmu$ and protein fold change was weakly negative ($r = -0.083$), in sharp contrast to the strong transcriptional correlation ($r = +0.419$) observed for the same gene set.
* **Transcript--Protein Decoupling:** The direct correlation between mRNA fold changes and protein fold changes across matched conditions was essentially zero ($r = +0.020, \, n = 799$).

These results align with the experimental findings of \citet{kochanowski2013}, who showed that metabolic adjustments under catabolic limitation are predominantly coordinated through post-translational metabolite-protein interactions and uncoordinated translation. Our findings indicate that cells transcribe metabolic enzymes to maintain the capacity for flux rerouting while buffering translational execution until metabolic bottlenecks arise.

# 6. Discussion

## 6.1. A Multiscale Perspective on Metabolic Curvature

The theoretical and computational findings presented here demonstrate that parametric flux balance models possess an intrinsic differential geometry governed by active-set switching complexes. The three primary sensitivity objects studied across mathematical programming and systems biology—discrete active-set jump tensors, continuous mollified curvatures, and integrated trajectory metrics—are manifestations of the same underlying matrix-valued Radon measure evaluated across different resolution scales:
\begin{equation}
\mu \;=\; D^2 u \;\xleftarrow[\sigma \to 0]{} \; \mu_{h, \sigma} = \mu_h * \varphi_\sigma \;\xrightarrow[\int \cdot \, dt]{} \; \kmu.
\end{equation}

Under mesh refinement, the atomic measure converges weakly to the continuous curvature density (Theorem~\ref{thm:Bprime}), but total-variation convergence fails generically due to triangulation anisotropy (Remark~\ref{rem:tvcounter}). A smooth representation of metabolic curvature is physically meaningful only within the intermediate resolution window $h \ll \sigma \ll L_{\mathrm{var}}$. Beyond this window, the smooth model breaks down, and the system is properly described by its discrete active-set atoms.

## 6.2. Biological Implications of the Visibility Dichotomy

Theorem~\ref{thm:coupling} establishes an exact mathematical separation between an organism's objective fitness landscape and its internal flux response. While the value function $\Phi(\theta)$ is single-valued and tie-break free, its second derivative contracts the internal flux measures ($D^2 \Phi = \sum c_r D^2 v^*_r$), projecting all degenerate internal reroutings to zero. 

Because cells routinely exploit alternative metabolic pathways to maintain growth during nutrient transitions, the vast majority of metabolic reroutings are objective-invisible. This explains why gene expression changes correlate with the flux-layer metric $\kmu$ ($r = +0.395$) but show no association with value-layer derivatives ($r = +0.032$). Transcriptional regulation is organized around internal network flexibility rather than the net growth rate.

## 6.3. Methodological Limitations

Several methodological considerations qualify our findings:
1. **Model Scope:** While our mathematical theorems hold for all multi-parametric linear programs, our empirical validations were conducted in *Escherichia coli*. Validating the universality of the proteomic buffering null across eukaryotes remains an important objective.
2. **Selection Dependence:** Although the value layer $\Phi(\theta)$ is strictly canonical, the flux layer $v^*(\theta)$ inherently depends on tie-breaking protocols across degenerate optimal faces. While we demonstrated that gene rankings remain stable ($\rho_S \ge 0.998$) across five distinct selection rules (Table~\ref{tab:v8}), the development of selection-free exterior algebra formulations represents an active area of inquiry.
3. **Cross-Platform Discrepancies:** While the association replicated across microarray starvation and diauxic experiments, cross-platform evaluations using PRECISE RNA-seq datasets \citep{sastry2019} yielded non-significant correlations ($r = -0.044$). This discrepancy indicates that the signal is specific to nutrient-exhaustion transitions rather than steady-state growth on alternative carbon sources.

# 7. Methods

## 7.1. Three-Stage Lexicographic Optimization Engine

To obtain unique, continuous, and piecewise-affine flux trajectories, all parametric linear programs were evaluated using a three-stage lexicographic solver. Let $x = (v, f, r)$ denote the flux vector decomposed into split forward and reverse components ($v = f - r$, with $f, r \ge 0$). The three sequential optimization stages are defined as:
\begin{align}
\text{Stage 1 (Biomass Maximization):} \quad & \mu^* = \max_{v} \; c_{\mathrm{bio}}^\top v \quad \text{s.t.} \quad Sv = 0, \quad \ell(\theta) \le v \le u(\theta), \label{eq:lex1} \\
\text{Stage 2 (Parsimonious Flux):} \quad & s_2^* = \min_{f, r} \; \sum_{j=1}^m (f_j + r_j) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \eqref{eq:lex1}, \quad c_{\mathrm{bio}}^\top v \ge \mu^*(1 - \varepsilon_\mu), \label{eq:lex2} \\
\text{Stage 3 (Deterministic Tie-Break):} \quad & v^*(\theta) = \arg\min_{v} \; w^\top v \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \eqref{eq:lex2}, \quad \sum_{j=1}^m (f_j + r_j) \le s_2^*(1 + \varepsilon_s), \label{eq:lex3}
\end{align}
where $\varepsilon_\mu = \varepsilon_s = 10^{-9}$ represent numerical relaxation tolerances. The deterministic weight vector $w \in \RR^m$ was sampled from a uniform distribution $w_j \sim U(0.5, 1.5)$ using fixed seed 20240901. All linear programs were solved using the HiGHS interior-point and simplex libraries \citep{huangfu2018} via `scipy.optimize.linprog` in cold-started configurations.

## 7.2. Data Provenance and Curation

Genome-scale metabolic reconstructions for *E. coli*—iJO1366 (2,583 reactions, 1,366 genes) \citep{orth2011} and iML1515 (2,712 reactions, 1,516 genes) \citep{monk2017}—were retrieved from the BiGG Models repository.

Microarray expression data were obtained from the Many Microbe Microarrays Database (M3D) Build 6 \citep{faith2008}, comprising 4,297 gene probes across 907 uniformly normalized Affymetrix arrays. Log-fold changes were calculated relative to wild-type log-phase growth in MOPS minimal glucose medium. RNA-seq data were retrieved from the PRECISE compendium \citep{sastry2019} (transcripts per million, TPM). Absolute protein copy numbers were acquired from the PaxDb integrated database \citep{wang2015}, and condition-dependent quantitative proteomic measurements were taken from \citet{schmidt2016} across 22 experimental conditions.

## 7.3. Statistical Analysis

The primary predictor variable was the base-10 logarithm $\log_{10}(\kmu)$, reflecting the log-normal distribution of the measure over five orders of magnitude. The primary response variable was the maximum absolute $\log_2$-fold change across experimental time points. Reported metrics include Pearson correlation coefficients ($r$) with analytical $p$-values, Spearman rank coefficients ($\rho_S$), partial correlation coefficients residualizing baseline expression, non-parametric two-sided permutation tests ($10^5$ resamples), and bootstrap $95\%$ confidence intervals ($10^4$ iterations). Decile contrasts were evaluated via one-sided Mann--Whitney $U$ tests. All computational results, analysis scripts, and reproducibility pipelines are deposited in our public repository.

# Appendix B: Mathematical Proofs

## B.1. Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:alex}

Let the primal parametric linear program be defined as:
\begin{equation}
\Phi(\theta) = \max_{v} \left\{ c^\top v : Sv = 0, \; \ell(\theta) \le v \le u(\theta) \right\},
\end{equation}
where $\ell(\theta) = \ell_0 + L\theta$ and $u(\theta) = u_0 + U\theta$ are affine maps. The dual linear program is:
\begin{equation}
\min_{y, w^+, w^-} \left\{ u(\theta)^\top w^+ - \ell(\theta)^\top w^- : S^\top y + w^+ - w^- = c, \; w^+ \ge 0, \; w^- \ge 0 \right\}.
\end{equation}
The dual feasible region $\mathcal{D} = \{ (y, w^+, w^-) : S^\top y + w^+ - w^- = c, \; w^\pm \ge 0 \}$ is a convex polyhedron that is entirely independent of the parameter vector $\theta$. By strong duality, because the primal problem is feasible and bounded for all $\theta \in \Theta$, the optimal value $\Phi(\theta)$ equals the dual objective. 

Because the dual objective is linear in $(w^+, w^-)$ for any fixed $\theta$, the optimum over the polyhedron $\mathcal{D}$ is achieved at one of its finitely many extreme vertices $\{ (y_k, w^+_k, w^-_k) \}_{k=1}^K$:
\begin{equation}
\Phi(\theta) = \min_{k \in \{1, \dots, K\}} \left\{ u(\theta)^\top w^+_k - \ell(\theta)^\top w^-_k \right\}.
\end{equation}
Each term in the minimum is an affine function of $\theta$. As the pointwise minimum of a finite collection of affine functions, $\Phi(\theta)$ is concave, continuous, and piecewise affine on $\Theta$.

Consequently, the negated function $f(\theta) = -\Phi(\theta)$ is convex and continuous piecewise affine. By Alexandrov's theorem \citep{alexandrov1939}, every convex function possesses a symmetric, positive semidefinite matrix-valued Radon Hessian measure $D^2 f$. Because $\Phi(\theta)$ is uniquely determined by the objective and constraint boundaries, this measure is independent of any tie-breaking rule used to choose among alternative optimal flux vectors. $\blacksquare$

## B.2. Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:twolayer}

Let $f: \RR^p \to \RR$ be convex and continuous piecewise affine over a polyhedral complex $\CC = \{\tau\}$.

**(i) Codimension-One Strata:** Let $g_\tau = \left.\nabla f\right|_\tau$ denote the constant gradient of $f$ on cell $\tau$. For any smooth test function $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ and coordinate directions $i, j \in \{1, \dots, p\}$, the distributional second derivative satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\langle \partial_i \partial_j f, \varphi \rangle = \int_\Omega f \, \partial_i \partial_j \varphi \, \dvol = -\sum_{\tau \in \CC^{(p)}} (g_\tau)_j \int_{\partial \tau} \varphi \, (\nu_\tau)_i \, \mathrm{d}\HH^{p-1},
\end{equation}
where $\nu_\tau$ denotes the outward unit normal along the boundary $\partial \tau$. Outer boundary terms vanish because $\varphi$ is compactly supported in $\Omega$. Each interior facet $F \in \CC^{(p-1)}$ is shared by exactly two cells, $\tau^+$ and $\tau^-$, whose outward normals satisfy $\nu_{\tau^+} = -\nu_{\tau^-} = n_F$. Summing across cells yields:
\begin{equation}
\langle \partial_i \partial_j f, \varphi \rangle = \sum_{F \in \CC^{(p-1)}} \left( [g]_F \right)_j (n_F)_i \int_F \varphi \, \mathrm{d}\HH^{p-1},
\end{equation}
where $[g]_F = g_{\tau^+} - g_{\tau^-}$ is the gradient jump. 

Because $f$ is continuous across $F$, its directional derivatives along any tangent vector $t \parallel F$ must match: $\langle [g]_F, t \rangle = 0$. Hence, the vector $[g]_F$ is parallel to the unit conormal $n_F$, giving $[g]_F = [\partial_n f]_F \, n_F$. The distributional Hessian is therefore:
\begin{equation}
D^2 f = \sum_{F \in \CC^{(p-1)}} [\partial_n f]_F \, (n_F \otimes n_F) \, \HH^{p-1}|_F.
\end{equation}
Convexity guarantees that the scalar normal jump is non-negative: $[\partial_n f]_F \ge 0$.

Now consider an $\varepsilon$-ball $B_\varepsilon(v)$ centered at a codimension-two vertex $v \in \CC^{(p-2)}$. The singular mass enclosed in this ball is:
\begin{equation}
\|D^2 f\|_{\TV}(B_\varepsilon(v)) = \sum_{F \ni v} [\partial_n f]_F \, \HH^{p-1}(F \cap B_\varepsilon(v)).
\end{equation}
Because each facet $F$ is a flat $(p-1)$-dimensional polyhedral surface, $F \cap B_\varepsilon(v)$ is contained in a $(p-1)$-dimensional disk of radius $\varepsilon$, whose measure is bounded by $\omega_{p-1} \varepsilon^{p-1}$. Because the complex contains finitely many facets meeting at $v$, the total mass in $B_\varepsilon(v)$ scales as $O(\varepsilon^{p-1})$. For $p=2$, this mass scales as $O(\varepsilon)$, yielding an empirical log--log scaling slope of $1.000$, and contains no point atoms.

**(ii) Codimension-Two Monge--Ampère Atoms:** For a convex function $f$, the Monge--Ampère measure $\nu_f$ is defined for any Borel set $E \subset \RR^p$ by $\nu_f(E) = |\partial f(E)|$, where $\partial f(E) = \bigcup_{x \in E} \partial f(x)$ is the image of the subdifferential mapping, and $|\cdot|$ denotes Lebesgue measure \citep{gutierrez2001}.

* For an interior point $x \in \operatorname{int}(\tau)$, $\partial f(x) = \{g_\tau\}$, which has Lebesgue measure zero.
* For a point on a codimension-one facet $x \in \operatorname{relint}(F)$, $\partial f(x) = \operatorname{conv}\{g_{\tau^-}, g_{\tau^+}\}$, which is a one-dimensional line segment of measure zero in $\RR^p$ ($p \ge 2$).
* For a codimension-two vertex $v \in \CC^{(p-2)}$, the subdifferential is the convex hull of the gradients of all maximal cells containing $v$:
\begin{equation}
\partial f(v) = \operatorname{conv} \{ g_\tau : \tau \ni v \}.
\end{equation}
For $p=2$, when three or more distinct chambers meet at $v$, this convex hull forms a two-dimensional polygon in gradient space with non-zero area. Consequently, the Monge--Ampère measure contains a discrete point mass at $v$:
\begin{equation}
\nu_f(\{v\}) = \bigl| \operatorname{conv} \{ \nabla f(\tau) : \tau \ni v \} \bigr|,
\end{equation}
which corresponds to the area of the normal fan. $\blacksquare$

## B.3. Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:dualface}

Let $g(\theta) = -\Phi(\theta)$ be the convex piecewise-affine negated value function. As shown in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:alex}, $g(\theta)$ can be expressed as the pointwise maximum of affine dual functions:
\begin{equation}
g(\theta) = \max_{k \in \{1, \dots, K\}} C_k(\theta), \quad \text{where} \quad C_k(\theta) = \ell(\theta)^\top w^-_k - u(\theta)^\top w^+_k.
\end{equation}
With affine parameter dependencies $u(\theta) = u_0 + U\theta$ and $\ell(\theta) = \ell_0 + L\theta$, the gradient of the $k$-th affine piece is:
\begin{equation}
\nabla C_k = L^\top w^-_k - U^\top w^+_k.
\end{equation}
When parameters enter solely through upper bounds ($L = 0$), this simplifies to $\nabla C_k = -U^\top w^+_k$.

At a codimension-two vertex $\theta_v$, let $\mathcal{K}(\theta_v) \subset \{1, \dots, K\}$ denote the indices of the affine pieces that attain the maximum: $C_k(\theta_v) = g(\theta_v)$ for all $k \in \mathcal{K}(\theta_v)$. By the subdifferential calculus for the maximum of convex functions \citep{rockafellar1970}:
\begin{equation}
\partial g(\theta_v) = \operatorname{conv} \{ \nabla C_k : k \in \mathcal{K}(\theta_v) \}.
\end{equation}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:twolayer}(ii), the Monge--Ampère atom equals the volume of this set:
\begin{equation}
\det D^2(-\Phi)(\{\theta_v\}) = |\partial g(\theta_v)| = \bigl| \operatorname{conv}\{\nabla C_k : k \in \mathcal{K}(\theta_v)\} \bigr|.
\end{equation}
The indices $k \in \mathcal{K}(\theta_v)$ correspond to the extreme vertices of the dual optimal face at $\theta_v$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}^*(\theta_v) = \arg\min_{(y, w^+, w^-) \in \mathcal{D}} \left\{ u(\theta_v)^\top w^+ - \ell(\theta_v)^\top w^- \right\}.
\end{equation}
Because the mapping $(y, w^+, w^-) \mapsto U^\top w^+$ is linear, it maps the convex dual optimal face $\mathcal{D}^*(\theta_v)$ directly to the subdifferential polygon $\operatorname{conv}\{\nabla C_k\}$:
\begin{equation}
\partial g(\theta_v) = -U^\top \left( \operatorname{proj}_{w^+} \mathcal{D}^*(\theta_v) \right).
\end{equation}
Because reflection preserves Euclidean volume, the area of the normal fan equals the projected area of the dual optimal face under $\theta \mapsto U^\top y$. $\blacksquare$

## B.4. Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:lex}

We establish that the three-stage lexicographic optimization problem defines a single-valued, continuous, and piecewise-affine flux function $v^*(\theta)$.

1. **Stage 1:** The optimal value function $\mu^*(\theta) = \max \{ c_{\mathrm{bio}}^\top v : Sv = 0, \, \ell(\theta) \le v \le u(\theta) \}$ is continuous and piecewise affine by Proposition~\ref{prop:alex}. The optimal face mapping:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{F}_1(\theta) = \left\{ v \in \RR^m : Sv = 0, \; \ell(\theta) \le v \le u(\theta), \; c_{\mathrm{bio}}^\top v \ge \mu^*(\theta)(1 - \varepsilon_\mu) \right\}
\end{equation}
is a non-empty, compact, polyhedral multifunction with closed graph.
2. **Stage 2:** The second stage minimizes total absolute flux over $\mathcal{F}_1(\theta)$:
\begin{equation}
s_2^*(\theta) = \min_{v, f, r} \left\{ \sum_{j=1}^m (f_j + r_j) : v \in \mathcal{F}_1(\theta), \; v = f - r, \; f \ge 0, \; r \ge 0 \right\}.
\end{equation}
Because the constraints are affine in $(v, f, r)$ and $\theta$, $s_2^*(\theta)$ is the optimal value function of a multi-parametric linear program with an affine right-hand side. Hence, $s_2^*(\theta)$ is continuous and piecewise affine. The stage-2 optimal face:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{F}_2(\theta) = \left\{ (v, f, r) : v \in \mathcal{F}_1(\theta), \; v = f - r, \; f, r \ge 0, \; \sum_{j=1}^m (f_j + r_j) \le s_2^*(\theta)(1 + \varepsilon_s) \right\}
\end{equation}
is a non-empty, compact, polyhedral multifunction.
3. **Stage 3:** The third stage minimizes $w^\top v$ over $\mathcal{F}_2(\theta)$. Because the weight vector $w \sim U(0.5, 1.5)$ is drawn from a continuous distribution, the probability that $w$ is normal to any edge of the polyhedral face $\mathcal{F}_2(\theta)$ is zero. Thus, the minimizer $v^*(\theta) = \arg\min_{v \in \mathcal{F}_2(\theta)} w^\top v$ is unique for almost all $w$. By multi-parametric linear programming theory \citep{borrelli2003}, the unique optimizer of an LP with affine parameter dependence is continuous and piecewise affine over a finite polyhedral chamber complex covering $\Theta$. $\blacksquare$

## B.5. Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:coupling}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:lex}, the lexicographic optimal flux $v^*(\theta)$ is continuous and piecewise affine. Because $\Phi(\theta) = c^\top v^*(\theta)$ holds pointwise for all $\theta \in \Theta$, this identity extends to distributional derivatives. Differentiation of distributions is linear; applying the distributional Laplacian or Hessian operator $D^2$ yields:
\begin{equation}
D^2 \Phi \;=\; D^2 \left( \sum_{r=1}^m c_r v^*_r \right) \;=\; \sum_{r=1}^m c_r \, D^2 v^*_r.
\end{equation}
Restricting this measure to an interior codimension-one facet $F \in \CC^{(p-1)}$ with conormal $n_F$ yields the jump relation:
\begin{equation}
[\nabla \Phi]_F = \sum_{r=1}^m c_r [\nabla v^*_r]_F = c^\top [\nabla v^*]_F.
\end{equation}
Taking the tensor product with $n_F$ and multiplying by the facet area $|F|$ recovers the crease measure jump identity:
\begin{equation}
\Delta \Phi'(t_k) = c^\top \Delta v'(t_k).
\end{equation}
Applying the Cauchy--Schwarz or Hölder inequality directly gives:
\begin{equation}
|\Delta \Phi'(t_k)| = |c^\top \Delta v'(t_k)| \le \|c\|_\infty \|\Delta v'(t_k)\|_1.
\end{equation}

**(i) Visibility Dichotomy:** If the optimal basic solution is uniquely determined on both sides of facet $F$, the gradient $\nabla \Phi|_\tau = -U^\top y^*_\tau$ is determined by the unique dual basic solution. Transversality ensures that $y^*_{\tau^+} \neq y^*_{\tau^-}$, yielding $\Delta \Phi' \neq 0$. Conversely, if alternative optimal basic solutions exist, the primal flux vector $v^*(\theta)$ can switch extreme vertices within the optimal face $\mathcal{F}_2(\theta)$ without altering the objective value. Along such degenerate transitions, $c^\top \Delta v' = 0$ while $\Delta v' \neq 0$.

**(ii) Sparse Objective Annihilation:** If the objective is defined entirely by biomass ($c = \gamma e_{\mathrm{bio}}$), substituting into the coupling identity gives:
\begin{equation}
D^2 \Phi = \gamma D^2 v^*_{\mathrm{bio}}.
\end{equation}
Because the biomass reaction vector contains non-zero entries only at the biomass index ($c_r = 0$ for all $r \neq \mathrm{bio}$), the value-layer measure contracts to the single scalar biomass component, decoupling from all internal reaction measures. $\blacksquare$

## B.6. Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:semiconvex}

A function $f: \Omega \to \RR$ is $\lambda$-semiconvex if $x \mapsto f(x) + \frac{\lambda}{2} |x|^2$ is convex. In the language of distributions, this requires that:
\begin{equation}
D^2 f + \lambda I \, \dvol \;\succeq\; 0
\end{equation}
in the sense of positive semidefinite matrix-valued measures.

Let $f$ be continuous and piecewise affine. By Proposition~\ref{prop:twolayer}, its distributional Hessian $D^2 f$ is supported entirely on the codimension-one skeleton $\CC^{(p-1)}$:
\begin{equation}
D^2 f = \sum_{F \in \CC^{(p-1)}} [\partial_n f]_F \, (n_F \otimes n_F) \, \HH^{p-1}|_F.
\end{equation}
Thus, $D^2 f$ is mutually singular with respect to the $p$-dimensional Lebesgue measure $\dvol$. 

The measure $D^2 f + \lambda I \dvol$ decomposes into two mutually singular components: an absolutely continuous part $\lambda I \dvol$ and a singular part $D^2 f$. For this sum to be positive semidefinite, both components must be positive semidefinite independently. Consequently, the singular component must satisfy:
\begin{equation}
D^2 f \succeq 0,
\end{equation}
which implies that $[\partial_n f]_F \ge 0$ along every facet $F$. A continuous piecewise-affine function whose normal gradient jumps are everywhere non-negative is convex. Thus, $f$ is $\lambda$-semiconvex for some finite $\lambda$ if and only if it is convex ($\lambda = 0$).

To demonstrate this breakdown numerically, consider the OR-plus-capacity value function $\Phi(\theta_1, \theta_2) = \min(\max(\theta_1, \theta_2), K)$. The non-convex ridge along the diagonal $\theta_1 = \theta_2$ exhibits a negative normal jump. Approximating this ridge across a discrete mesh of spacing $h$ yields an effective curvature scaling as $-1/(2h)$. As $h \to 0$, this negative curvature diverges, violating semiconvexity for any finite $\lambda$. $\blacksquare$

## B.7. Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:maatom}

Let $f: \RR^2 \to \RR$ be convex and piecewise affine. Consider a generic codimension-two vertex $v$ where three chambers meet with constant gradients $n_1, n_2, n_3$ ordered cyclically. The gradient jumps across the three incident facets are:
\begin{equation}
j_1 = n_2 - n_1, \quad j_2 = n_3 - n_2, \quad j_3 = n_1 - n_3.
\end{equation}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:twolayer}(ii), the Monge--Ampère point atom at $v$ equals the area of the normal fan $\operatorname{conv}\{n_1, n_2, n_3\}$.

The area of the triangle spanned by vertices $n_1, n_2, n_3$ in $\RR^2$ is given by half the absolute value of the determinant of its edge vectors:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Area}(\operatorname{conv}\{n_1, n_2, n_3\}) = \tfrac{1}{2} |\det(n_2 - n_1, \, n_3 - n_1)|.
\end{equation}
Substituting $n_3 - n_1 = -(j_1 + j_2)$ yields:
\begin{equation}
\det(j_1, -(j_1 + j_2)) = -\det(j_1, j_2).
\end{equation}
Thus, the atom weight is:
\begin{equation}
\det D^2 f(\{v\}) = \tfrac{1}{2} |\det(j_1, j_2)| = \tfrac{1}{2} |j_1| |j_2| \sin \phi,
\end{equation}
where $\phi \in (0, \pi)$ is the angle between the jump vectors $j_1$ and $j_2$.

Approximating this atom by the product of the jump norms $|j_1| |j_2|$ yields the ratio:
\begin{equation}
\frac{|j_1| |j_2|}{\frac{1}{2} |j_1| |j_2| \sin \phi} = \frac{2}{\sin \phi}.
\end{equation}
Because $\sin \phi \le 1$ for all $\phi$, this ratio satisfies $2/\sin \phi \ge 2$, with equality if and only if $\sin \phi = 1$ ($\phi = \pi/2$, corresponding to orthogonal jumps). $\blacksquare$

## B.8. Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:Bprime}

Let $\Omega \subset \RR^p$ be compact, $u \in C^2(\Omega; \RR^m)$, and let $\{\mathcal{T}_h\}$ be a shape-regular family of conforming triangulations with $h = \max_{\tau \in \mathcal{T}_h} \operatorname{diam}(\tau) \to 0$. Let $u_h$ be the piecewise-affine Lagrange interpolant of $u$.

**(i) Atomic Support:** Because $u_h$ is continuous and piecewise affine on each simplex $\tau \in \mathcal{T}_h$, Proposition~\ref{prop:twolayer}(i) implies that its distributional second derivative is concentrated on the $(p-1)$-skeleton $\mathcal{T}_h^{(p-1)}$:
\begin{equation}
\mu_h = D^2 u_h = \sum_{F \in \mathcal{T}_h^{(p-1)}} [\nabla u_h]_F \otimes n_F \, |F| \, \delta_F.
\end{equation}
Lower-dimensional simplices have Hausdorff dimension $\le p-2$, and their $(p-1)$-dimensional Hausdorff measure is zero.

**(ii) Weak Convergence:** For any test function $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$, the definition of distributional derivatives gives:
\begin{equation}
\langle \mu_h, \varphi \rangle = \int_\Omega u_h \, D^2 \varphi \, \dvol, \quad \text{and} \quad \langle \mu, \varphi \rangle = \int_\Omega u \, D^2 \varphi \, \dvol.
\end{equation}
Subtracting the two expressions yields:
\begin{equation}
|\langle \mu_h - \mu, \varphi \rangle| = \left| \int_\Omega (u_h - u) \, D^2 \varphi \, \dvol \right| \le \|u_h - u\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)} \|D^2 \varphi\|_{L^1(\Omega)}.
\end{equation}
By standard finite-element interpolation estimates on shape-regular meshes \citep{brenner2008}, there exists a constant $C$ such that:
\begin{equation}
\|u - u_h\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)} \le C h^2 |u|_{C^2(\Omega)}.
\end{equation}
Substituting this estimate yields:
\begin{equation}
|\langle \mu_h - \mu, \varphi \rangle| \le C h^2 |u|_{C^2(\Omega)} \|D^2 \varphi\|_{L^1(\Omega)} \xrightarrow{h \to 0} 0.
\end{equation}
Convergence in the Kantorovich--Rubinstein norm $\|\mu_h - \mu\|_{\KR} \to 0$ follows from the uniform equicontinuity of the test class $\{\phi : \Lip(\phi) \le 1, \, \|\phi\|_\infty \le 1\}$ on compact $\Omega$ via the Arzelà--Ascoli theorem.

**(iii) Total Variation Bounds:** On each simplex $\tau$, the gradient of the interpolant satisfies $\|\nabla u_h - \nabla u\|_{L^\infty(\tau)} \le C h |u|_{C^2(\tau)}$. Across any facet $F = \tau^+ \cap \tau^-$, the gradient jump satisfies $|[\nabla u_h]_F| \le 2 C h |u|_{C^2}$. The total variation is bounded by:
\begin{equation}
\|\mu_h\|_{\TV} = \sum_{F} |[\nabla u_h]_F| |F| \le 2 C h |u|_{C^2} \sum_F |F|.
\end{equation}
By shape regularity and quasi-uniformity, $\sum_F |F| \le C' |\Omega| h^{-1}$, which implies:
\begin{equation}
\sup_{h > 0} \|\mu_h\|_{\TV} \le 2 C C' |\Omega| |u|_{C^2} < \infty.
\end{equation}
Lower semicontinuity of the total variation norm under weak convergence guarantees that $\liminf_{h \to 0} \|\mu_h\|_{\TV} \ge \|\mu\|_{\TV}$. The generic failure of $\|\mu_h\|_{\TV} \to \|\mu\|_{\TV}$ is proved by the explicit calculation in Remark~\ref{rem:tvcounter}.

Along a one-dimensional path ($p=1$), let $a = t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_N = b$ be a mesh. If $u$ is convex, $u_h$ is convex, and its derivative $u_h'$ is non-decreasing. The total variation telescopes:
\begin{equation}
\|\mu_h\|_{\TV} = \sum_{k=1}^{N-1} (u_h'(t_k^+) - u_h'(t_k^-)) = u_h'(t_{N-1}^+) - u_h'(t_0^+) \xrightarrow{h \to 0} u'(b) - u'(a) = \int_a^b u''(t) \, dt = \|\mu\|_{\TV}.
\end{equation}

**(iv) Strong $L^1$ Dual-Cell Reconstruction:** On a structured uniform grid in $\RR^p$, let $\tau$ be a polyhedral cell of measure $|\tau| = h^p$. Assign each interior facet $F \subset \partial \tau$ a weighting factor $\omega_{F, \tau} = 1/2$. The cell-averaged reconstructed density is:
\begin{equation}
\rho_h(\tau) = \frac{1}{h^p} \sum_{F \subset \partial \tau} \frac{1}{2} A_F.
\end{equation}
Taylor expansion of $u \in C^3$ around the cell centroid $x_\tau$ shows that the linear gradient terms cancel across opposing parallel facets due to central symmetry. The second-order terms recover the exact smooth Hessian $D^2 u(x_\tau)$, with the truncation error bounded by $C h |u|_{C^3}$. Integrating over all cells yields:
\begin{equation}
\|\rho_h - D^2 u\|_{L^1(\Omega)} \le \sum_{\tau} |\tau| \, C h |u|_{C^3(\tau)} \le C h |\Omega| |u|_{C^3} = O(h) \xrightarrow{h \to 0} 0.
\end{equation}
Consequently, $\|\rho_h\|_{L^1(\Omega)} \to \|D^2 u\|_{L^1(\Omega)} = \|\mu\|_{\TV}$.

**(v) Resolution Window:** Mollifying $\mu_h$ with a kernel $\varphi_\sigma(x) = \sigma^{-p} \phi(x/\sigma)$ yields the regularized density:
\begin{equation}
\mu_{h, \sigma}(x) = (\mu_h * \varphi_\sigma)(x) = \langle \mu_h, \, \varphi_\sigma(x - \cdot) \rangle.
\end{equation}
By the triangle inequality, the approximation error decomposes into:
\begin{equation}
\|\mu_{h, \sigma} - D^2 u\|_{L^1} \le \|\mu_h * \varphi_\sigma - \mu * \varphi_\sigma\|_{L^1} + \|\mu * \varphi_\sigma - D^2 u\|_{L^1}.
\end{equation}
The first term reflects discrete sampling error, which is bounded by $C (h / \sigma) |u|_{C^2}$ using the weak convergence estimate from part (ii). The second term reflects kernel smoothing error, bounded by $C \sigma \|D^3 u\|_{L^1} = C (\sigma / L_{\mathrm{var}}) |u|_{C^2}$. Thus:
\begin{equation}
\|\mu_{h, \sigma} - D^2 u\|_{L^1} \le C |u|_{C^2} \left( \frac{h}{\sigma} + \frac{\sigma}{L_{\mathrm{var}}} \right).
\end{equation}
This total error vanishes asymptotically if and only if both $h / \sigma \to 0$ and $\sigma / L_{\mathrm{var}} \to 0$, establishing the resolution window $h \ll \sigma \ll L_{\mathrm{var}}$. $\blacksquare$

## B.9. Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:seclaw}

Let $S_u = \{ (x, u(x)) : x \in \Omega \} \subset \RR^{p+1}$ denote the graph of $u \in C^2(\Omega)$, oriented by the upward unit normal:
\begin{equation}
N(x) = \frac{(-\nabla u(x), \, 1)}{\sqrt{1 + |\nabla u(x)|^2}}.
\end{equation}
The classical Gauss map $\mathcal{G}: S_u \to \mathbb{S}^p$ maps each surface point $(x, u(x))$ to its unit normal $N(x)$.

The differential of the Gauss map corresponds to the shape operator, whose determinant with respect to the surface area element $dA = \sqrt{1 + |\nabla u|^2} \, \dvol$ is the intrinsic Gaussian curvature:
\begin{equation}
K(x) = \frac{\det D^2 u(x)}{(1 + |\nabla u(x)|^2)^{(p+2)/2}}.
\end{equation}
The Jacobian of the normal map with respect to the parameter base area element $\dvol$ is:
\begin{equation}
|\operatorname{Jac} \mathcal{G}|_{\mathrm{base}} = K(x) \frac{dA}{\dvol} = \frac{\det D^2 u(x)}{(1 + |\nabla u(x)|^2)^{(p+2)/2}} \sqrt{1 + |\nabla u(x)|^2} = \frac{\det D^2 u(x)}{(1 + |\nabla u(x)|^2)^{(p+1)/2}}.
\end{equation}
For a two-dimensional parameter domain ($p=2$), this evaluates to:
\begin{equation}
|\operatorname{Jac} \mathcal{G}|_{\mathrm{base}} = \frac{\det D^2 u}{(1 + |\nabla u|^2)^{3/2}}.
\end{equation}

For a triangulated polyhedral graph surface $S_{u_h}$, the angle defect at vertex $v$ equals the spherical area of the normal fan spanned by the unit normals of the incident facets \citep{regge1961, cheeger1984}:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{defect}(v) = \operatorname{Area}_{\mathbb{S}^2}(\mathcal{G}(v)).
\end{equation}
By Cheeger, Müller, and Schrader's convergence theorem for curvature measures of piecewise-flat spaces \citep{cheeger1984}, the vertex defect measure converges weakly to the spherical image measure of the smooth Gauss map:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{v} \operatorname{defect}(v) \, \delta_v \rightharpoonup |\operatorname{Jac} \mathcal{G}|_{\mathrm{base}} \, \dvol = \frac{\det D^2 u}{(1 + |\nabla u|^2)^{3/2}} \, \dvol.
\end{equation}
Comparing this density to the intrinsic Gaussian curvature $K = \det D^2 u / (1 + |\nabla u|^2)^2$ yields the ratio:
\begin{equation}
\frac{|\operatorname{Jac} \mathcal{G}|_{\mathrm{base}}}{K} = \frac{\frac{\det D^2 u}{(1 + |\nabla u|^2)^{3/2}}}{\frac{\det D^2 u}{(1 + |\nabla u|^2)^2}} = \sqrt{1 + |\nabla u|^2} = \sec \theta_{\mathrm{tilt}},
\end{equation}
where $\theta_{\mathrm{tilt}}$ is the angle between the surface normal $N(x)$ and the vertical coordinate axis $e_{p+1}$. Recovering intrinsic Gaussian curvature therefore requires dividing by this secant factor. $\blacksquare$

**Abstract**

Parametric flux balance analysis defines a piecewise-affine map from environmental and genetic parameters onto optimal fluxes. We show that the distributional second derivative of this map is a matrix-valued Radon measure supported on the codimension-one strata of the active-set complex. This measure is the canonical carrier of the network’s rerouting geometry: loop holonomy scales linearly with loop size (empirical slope \(1.00\); smooth maps scale quadratically), and second-order response mass concentrates (\(93.4\)–\(100.0\%\) per boundary-crossing sweep) on active-set switches. We prove a refinement–resolution bridge: under mesh refinement the atomic measure converges weakly to the smooth curvature density, dual-cell reconstruction converges strongly in \(L^1\), and an exact counter-example calculus shows that total-variation convergence fails in general. A resolution parameter \(\sigma\) separates the discrete and smooth regimes inside the measured window \(h\ll\sigma\ll L_{\mathrm{var}}\) (\(L_{\mathrm{var}}\) being the characteristic scale of smooth variation). From the measure we derive a gene-level sensitivity metric \(\kappa_\mu\) that, on *E. coli* (M3D microarrays, \(n=424\)), predicts carbon-depletion transcriptional response (\(r=+0.395\), \(p=2.6\times10^{-17}\); partial \(r=+0.269\) after reference-level control; metric-invariant \(\rho=0.99998\) under the lexicographic definition; tie-break-robust across five selection rules; event measures stabilizing at the Glivenko–Cantelli rate). The association does not propagate to the protein layer: cells transcribe rerouting potential while buffering its translation. Double-knockout epistasis aligns with active-set structure (\(\rho_S=0.865\)), and genotype loops close with phenotypic memory (\(66\%\) non-reverting). The framework unifies three distinct sensitivity objects at different resolutions.

**Keywords:** flux balance analysis; discrete curvature; active set; parametric linear programming; flux rerouting; epistasis

# 1. Introduction

Let a metabolic network with \(m\) reactions be operated by flux-balance analysis with parameters \(\theta\in\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^p\) (uptake bounds, knockdown scalings) that enter the constraints affinely. The optimal flux map \(v:\Theta\to\mathbb{R}^m\) is continuous and piecewise affine, with breakpoints lying on the active-set strata of the constraint complex. Its distributional second derivative \(\mu=D^2v\) is therefore not a function but a measure—the discrete curvature carrier of the network’s rerouting geometry.

The manuscript establishes, in order:

(i) the atom structure and weak convergence of \(\mu_h\) under refinement (Theorem 1, §3), together with an exact counter-example calculus that delimits what is provable (weak convergence, strong cell-wise reconstruction) from what fails (total-variation convergence);

(ii) the two-layer separation: the codimension-one Hessian layer is the unbiased curvature estimator, while the codimension-two angle-defect layer converges to the Gauss-map area density with an exact sec-law bias (Proposition 4);

(iii) the regime dichotomy: piecewise-affine maps with fixed kink complexes produce \(O(\varepsilon)\) loop holonomy (measured slope \(1.00\)), whereas smooth maps produce \(O(\varepsilon^2)\);

(iv) the value function \(\Phi=c_{\mathrm{bio}}^\top v^*\) is the canonical, tie-break-free carrier, with shadow-price jumps measurable to \(6\)–\(7\) digits against Danskin envelopes;

(v) the measure-derived metric \(\kappa_\mu\) predicts carbon-depletion transcriptional response (\(r=+0.395\)), generalizes to oxygen-limitation and carbon-switch trajectories with matched responses (\(r=+0.32\), \(+0.22\); §5.3), is robust to the tie-break convention (\(r\in[+0.386,+0.396]\) across five selection rules; §5.4), is buffered at the protein layer (§5), and its event measures stabilize at the Glivenko–Cantelli rate across random panels (§5.5).

Kochanowski et al. (2013) showed that global metabolic coordination under catabolic limitation is largely passive at the protein/metabolite level, driven by Crp and local metabolite concentrations. The present work asks a different question: whether a gene-specific, network-derived rerouting necessity predicts transcriptional response. We find that it does, and that the transcript-level association does not propagate to the protein layer—consistent with a model in which cells transcribe the potential for rerouting while buffering its translation.

# 2. The discrete curvature measure of parametric FBA

## 2.1 The atomic curvature measure

**Definition 1** (Atomic curvature measure). Let \(u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^m\) be continuous and piecewise affine on a polyhedral complex \(\mathcal{C}\) covering \(\Omega\). For each codimension-one facet \(F\) of \(\mathcal{C}\) shared by cells \(\tau^-\) and \(\tau^+\), let \([\nabla u]_F=\nabla u|_{\tau^+}-\nabla u|_{\tau^-}\), let \(n_F\) be the unit conormal oriented from \(\tau^-\) to \(\tau^+\), and let \(|F|\) be the facet measure. The atomic curvature measure is the matrix-valued Radon measure
\[
\mu=D^2u=\sum_F A_F\,\delta_F,\qquad
A_F=[\nabla u]_F\otimes n_F\,|F|\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times p\times p}.
\]

**Remark 1** (Support). The measure \(\mu\) is concentrated on the \((p-1)\)-skeleton. The \((p-2)\)-skeleton carries a different measure (the angle-defect layer, Proposition 4); the two must not be conflated. In dimension \(p=1\) the atoms are simply the slope jumps at breakpoints.

The measure-theoretic language is classical: matrix-valued Radon measures and their weak convergence, the Alexandrov Hessian of convex functions, the Monge–Ampère measure, and Regge-type defect measures for piecewise-flat curvature on the geometric side; chamber complexes, normal fans, and multi-parametric LP value functions on the polyhedral side. The Kantorovich–Rubinstein (flat) norm used in the refinement–resolution bridge is the standard optimal-transport flat norm.

## 2.2 The measure-theoretic sensitivity metric

**Definition 2** (\(\kappa_\mu\)). For a \(C^1\) parameter trajectory \(\theta(t)\), \(t\in[0,T]\), and its piecewise-affine optimal-flux response \(v(\theta(t))\), let \(\{t_e\}_e\) be the event times at which the trajectory crosses active-set strata, with atoms \(A_e=A_{F(e)}\) evaluated on the crossed facet. Define
\[
\kappa_\mu(g)=\frac1T\sum_e\|A_e\|\,w_e(g),
\]
where \(\|\cdot\|\) is the entrywise \(1\)-norm on \(\mathbb{R}^{m\times p\times p}\) and \(w_e(g)\in\{0,1\}\) selects those events whose crossed stratum changes the activity of reactions assigned to gene \(g\) (maximum over its reactions, according to the GPR association).

**Remark 2** (\(\kappa_\mu\) is the safe regime of the reconstruction). The metric \(\kappa_\mu\) integrates measure mass along a one-dimensional trajectory—the telescoping regime in which total variation and mass are automatically consistent (see §3). Its resolution independence (measured mass \(288.77\) stable under \(4\times\)/\(8\times\) trajectory refinement) is exactly the \(L^1\)-reconstruction regime of Theorem 1(iv). Its per-reaction masses are the components of the flux-layer crease measure whose objective contraction is \(D^2\Phi\) (Theorem 3): the metric is the primal layer of the canonical object.

**Remark 3** (Status of the definition, tie-breaking, and layer assignment). The definition of \(\kappa_\mu\) is final for the empirical claims of this manuscript. (i) The carrier is the flux-layer signed crease measure \(D^2v^*\) (Definition 1 applied to \(u=v^*\)) of the three-stage lexicographic optimal flux (§6); the lexicographic tie-break (biomass, then \(\ell_1\) flux, then seeded deterministic weights) is part of the metric’s definition, not an implementation detail—the flux layer is selection-dependent by construction, and the tie-break is declared rather than eliminated. (ii) The per-reaction mass is the unsigned total variation \(\frac1T\sum_e\|A_e\|_1\) along the parameter trajectory. (iii) The value layer \(D^2\Phi\) enters only as a derived diagnostic (Theorem 3: \(D^2\Phi=\sum_rc_r\,D^2v^*_r\)), never as the predictor: no claim of this manuscript attributes transcriptional response to Alexandrov curvature of the value function. (iv) The layer correlated with transcriptomics is the codimension-one Hessian/crease layer of the flux map—not the Monge–Ampère atom layer (Proposition 7), which requires concavity and serves as the dual-face diagnostic. Robustness to the declared selection is measured, not assumed: the within-layer rank identity \(\rho(\kappa_\mu,\kappa^{\mathrm{lex}})=0.99998\) (§5.1), the trajectory substitutions of §5.3 reproduce the association under the same declared selection on two further paths, and §5.4 holds the association stable (\(r\in[+0.386,+0.396]\), partial constant to three decimals) under four alternative stage-3 selection rules while the value layer stays exactly invariant.

## 2.3 The value-function layer (Alexandrov)

**Proposition 1** (Existence and tie-break-freeness). Let \(\Phi(\theta)=\max\{c^\top v:Sv=0,\,\ell(\theta)\le v\le u(\theta)\}\) with \(\ell,u\) affine, feasible and bounded. Then \(\Phi\) is concave (pointwise infimum of affine dual objectives over a \(\theta\)-independent dual feasible set), and \(-\Phi\) carries a symmetric matrix-valued Radon Hessian measure \(D^2(-\Phi)\) (Alexandrov), independent of the optimal-flux selection rule. Concavity fails in general under OR (isoenzyme) GPR rules for simultaneous multi-gene capacity vectors; single-gene axes, exchange-bound families, and AND-only subnetworks retain it. For OR-containing rules the value function remains continuous piecewise affine and carries the signed crease measure of Proposition 6; only the Monge–Ampère layer requires the concave class.

**Proposition 2** (Two-layer structure of \(D^2(-\Phi)\)). Let \(f\) be convex piecewise affine. (i) The singular part of \(D^2f\) is supported on the codimension-one facets, with density \((g^+-g^-)\otimes n=[\partial_nf]\,n\otimes n\) per unit facet measure; it has no atoms: the mass in an \(\varepsilon\)-neighbourhood of a codimension-two vertex is \(O(\varepsilon)\) (measured log–log slope \(1.000\)). (ii) The Monge–Ampère measure \(\det D^2f\) is atomic at codimension-two vertices, each atom equal to the volume of the normal fan \(|\operatorname{conv}\{\nabla f(\tau):\tau\ni\text{vertex}\}|\) (measured: constant \(0.5\) under \(\varepsilon\)-shrinking).

**Proposition 3** (Dual optimal face identity). At a codimension-two vertex \(\theta_v\) of a parametric-LP value function, the Monge–Ampère atom of \(-\Phi\) equals the area of the normal fan, and the fan is the image of the LP dual optimal face under \(\theta\mapsto U^\top y\):
\[
\det D^2(-\Phi)(\{\theta_v\})
=|\operatorname{conv}\{\nabla C_i\}|
=|\{U^\top y:(\lambda,y)\text{ optimal dual}\}|.
\]
Machine-verified on a two-parameter max-flow LP (the phenotype phase plane in miniature): vertex refined to machine precision by exact triple-tie solve, \(\Phi(\theta_v)\) matching the LP value to \(10^{-15}\), fan area \(0.235\) versus dual-face enumeration area \(0.235000068\) (\(16\) direction LPs over the dual optimal face).

**Remark 4** (Layer separation and PhPP positioning). The phenotype phase planes of Edwards–Ibarra–Palsson are the chamber complex of \(\Phi\); their sector corners are the atoms of the Monge–Ampère layer. The empirical \(\kappa_\mu\) of Definition 2 is a functional of the flux-map measure \(D^2v\) (Definition 1), which is tie-break-sensitive: under two lexicographic tie-breaks the value layer of a controlled follower-variable LP is invariant, while the flux layer’s second-difference mass moves by ten orders of magnitude (§4.1). The controlled relation between the value layer and the flux layer is a theorem (Theorem 3 and Corollary 1); the association results of §5 stand on the flux layer alone, which is where the evidence (§§5.1–5.2) actually lives.

## 2.4 The value–flux coupling

**Lemma 1** (Lex-selection regularity). Let \(\ell,u\) be continuous piecewise affine and let \(v^*(\theta)\) be the three-stage lexicographic optimal flux (§6). Then \(v^*\) is well-defined, continuous, and piecewise affine; its graph over each GPR cell is a finite polyhedral complex in \((v,\theta)\)-space (each lexicographic stage adjoins the graph of a piecewise-affine value function, which is polyhedral, and an injective polyhedral projection is piecewise affine). Measured: path-segment residuals \(\le8\times10^{-14}\) (§4.1); \(\Phi\) piecewise affine at \(4.2\times10^{-13}\) (§4.4).

**Theorem 3** (Value–flux crease coupling). \(\Phi=c^\top v^*\) pointwise; hence, as symmetric matrix-valued signed Radon measures on the parameter domain,
\[
D^2\Phi=\sum_rc_r\,D^2v^*_r.
\]
Along any piecewise-affine trajectory, at every crease time \(t_k\): \(\Delta\Phi'(t_k)=c^\top\Delta v'(t_k)\), and \(|\Delta\Phi'|\le\|c\|_\infty\|\Delta v'\|_1\).

(i) (Visibility dichotomy.) An event with unique optima on both sides is a transversal optimal-vertex switch and is necessarily objective-moving; objective-invisible events (\(c^\top\Delta v'=0\), \(\Delta v'\ne0\)) are exactly the degenerate reroutings inside \(\ge1\)-dimensional optimal faces (mask-type events). The tie-break sensitivity of the flux layer is concentrated in the invisible events; the \(c\)-contraction is tie-break-free because it annihilates the degenerate layer.

(ii) (Sparse-objective corollary.) If \(c=\gamma e_{\mathrm{bio}}\), then \(D^2\Phi=\gamma\,D^2v^*_{\mathrm{bio}}\): the value layer is the crease measure of the single biomass component, and per-gene attribution from the value layer is structurally impossible.

Machine-verified: identity error \(0.0\) at all \(12\) events and all \(4\) clusters of the iML1515 cut (\(\Phi=c^\top v^*\) to \(10^{-9}\); Fig. 2); \(11\) of \(12\) events objective-invisible, with flux \(\ell_1\) slope jumps up to \(1.6\times10^4\) against \(|c^\top\Delta v'|\le1.51\times10^{-7}\); \(150\) random dense-objective LPs to \(1.1\times10^{-13}\) (\(5\) events, all objective-moving—the dichotomy); the degenerate follower family \(60/60\) with invisible kinks at identity error \(2.0\times10^{-13}\); two-dimensional mixed second differences at crease-straddling boxes to \(6.4\times10^{-16}\).

**Proposition 6** (Semiconvexity collapse; signed layer for mixed GPRs). A continuous piecewise-affine function is semiconvex (respectively semiconcave) if and only if it is convex (respectively concave)—semiconvexity is not an available weakening for LP value functions (measured: the required constant blows up like \(1/(2h)\); \(\lambda\cdot h_{\max}=0.500\) numerically across \(\lambda=1\dots10^6\), the analytic law \(h_{\max}=1/(2\lambda)\)—equivalently \(\lambda h_{\max}=1/2\)—holding at every scale, with the numerical grid flooring above \(\lambda\approx10^7\)). Nevertheless every continuous piecewise-affine \(\Phi\), for any GPR structure, carries a finite signed symmetric Radon Hessian measure on its crease skeleton with per-crease sign flags: for the OR-plus-cap value function \(\min(\max(\theta_1,\theta_2),K)\), the diagonal crease density is positive semidefinite (eigenvalues \(\{0,\sqrt2\}\)) and the cap crease negative semidefinite (\(\{-1,0\}\)). Concavity is required only by the Monge–Ampère layer and the dual-face identity; the signed Hessian layer applies to all genes. Under multiplicative activity scaling with \(\ell\le0\le u\), \(\Phi=\Psi(a(\theta))\) with \(\Psi\) concave nondecreasing: AND-only (min-tree) GPRs retain concavity, top-level OR nodes can break it, and disaggregating isozymes into separate reactions restores affine bounds while replacing max-semantics by sum-semantics (capacity \(1\) versus \(2\) at full expression)—a different biological model, not a regularisation.

**Proposition 7** (Monge–Ampère atoms are determinants, not products). For convex piecewise-affine \(f\) on \(\mathbb{R}^2\) at a generic vertex with incident gradients \(n_1,n_2,n_3\) and codimension-one gradient jumps \(j_i\): the atom equals \(\tfrac12|\det(j_1,j_2)|=|\operatorname{conv}\{n_i\}|=\tfrac12|j_1||j_2|\sin\angle\); the product \(|j_1||j_2|\) overestimates by \(2/\sin\angle\ge2\), with equality if and only if the jumps are orthogonal (measured on \(400\) random max-of-affine vertices: determinant \(=\) fan area to \(8.9\times10^{-16}\); product/atom median \(3.296\), minimum exactly \(2.000\) (the orthogonal edge case; \(2.000031\) over random vertices); the \(2/\sin\angle\) law to \(2.5\times10^{-12}\); in \(p\) dimensions, atom \(=(1/p!)|\det(j_1,\dots,j_p)|\) for simplex fans).

**Remark 5** (Sign and normalisation conventions). Definition 1 applied to \(u=-\Phi\) is the signed crease measure of the value function: per facet \(F\), the density \(A_F=[\nabla(-\Phi)]_F\otimes n_F\,|F|\), a symmetric matrix whose eigenvalue signs flag the crease’s convexity/concavity (Proposition 6). This is the explicit extension of Alexandrov’s setting to arbitrary GPR structure: within the concave class \(-\Phi\) is convex, every \(A_F\succeq0\), and the measure coincides with the Alexandrov Hessian measure; outside it, the same definition produces the signed layer. All masses in this manuscript are unsigned total variations (entrywise \(1\)-norm for matrix atoms, trajectory sums for \(\kappa_\mu\)); Monge–Ampère atoms are unsigned \(\tfrac12|\det(j_1,j_2)|\) equal to the normal-fan volume, normalised to \((1/p!)|\det(j_1,\dots,j_p)|\) for simplex fans in dimension \(p\) (Proposition 7). Signed determinants appear only in the concave class, where they are nonnegative.

**Remark 6** (What this closes). Theorem 3 is the exact form of the value–flux layer relation: the empirical \(\kappa_\mu\) is the per-reaction total variation of the flux-layer crease measure whose objective contraction is \(D^2\Phi\)—the metric is the primal layer of the canonical object, justified by identity rather than by rank agreement.

## 2.5 The categorical reading, in brief

The definitions and results of §2 are measure-theoretic, and the empirical claims rest on them alone. The categorical reading summarised in this subsection motivates the active-set curvature interpretation and is developed in full—constructions, machine verifications, and proofs, with the proof status marked per result (complete for the gluing formula, the transport law, the composition and contraction layers, and the terminal-coalgebra theorem; machine-verified numerics; citation-level stratified descent; proof-sketch status for the homotopy-type-theoretic extension)—in a companion theory paper. None of the empirical results of this manuscript depends on it.

*Strata as a stratified base.* The lexicographic pFBA engine (§6.1) partitions parameter space into chambers on which the optimiser is affine; the codimension-one facets where the active set switches are exactly the facets that carry the atomic measure \(\mu\) of Definition 1. In the categorical reading, this partition is a stratified base and the optimiser organises the policy fibre stratum by stratum, as a stratified connection in the companion’s \(2\)-category \(\mathbf{StCon}(B)\): connection \(1\)-forms on each stratum, glued along the switching facets by a boundary transition satisfying a gauge matching condition, with a gluing theorem (proved there by reduction to stack-descent theory, and with its piecewise-holonomy consequences machine-verified in five loop geometries and three structure groups).

*The active-set bridge.* Two results of the companion connect this geometry to viability. First, on each constant-active-set stratum, a Fisher-minimal transport law defines the connection in closed form as a constrained projection with the Fisher–Rao metric; its curvature is the smooth avatar of the per-facet density that \(\mu\) concentrates on. Second, a small-loop viability–holonomy theorem bounds the endpoint erosion of a viability margin around a small parameter loop by the viability-weighted curvature: the worst case, over unit-area parameter bivectors and active viability margins, of the positive part of the margin’s change along the connection curvature, normalised by the margin itself. The per-pair form of \(\kappa_\mu\) is modelled on that object—the atomic crease measure is the measure-theoretic analogue of the curvature \(2\)-form, and the value–flux coupling (Theorem 3) supplies the analogue of the survival covectors; the precise discretisation correspondence (a domination or ratio-form identity between the two) is open.

*Regime delineation.* The two holonomy-scaling regimes of Proposition 5 are the measure-level image of the companion’s two loop regimes: the smooth arm (slope \(2.000\)) is where the companion’s quadratic small-loop law \(\kappa(a)=a^2\) and its Fisher-minimal transport law live; the piecewise-affine arm (slope \(1.00\)) is the companion’s wall-crossing regime, where a loop crossing a switching facet accumulates a boundary contribution at \(O(\varepsilon)\) per crossing (the companion’s pairs-crossing theorem).

*Division of labour.* The present paper states only this brief reading; the companion theory paper develops the SAVGS object, the gluing theorem and piecewise holonomy formula, the optic-composition semantics with its contraction analysis, the filtered-colimit construction of autocatalytic sets, and the homotopy-type-theoretic extension, with proofs and extensions.

# 3. The refinement–resolution bridge

Throughout this section \(\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^p\) is compact, \(p\ge1\), \(u\in C^2(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^m)\), \(\{\mathcal{T}_h\}\) is a shape-regular family of conforming triangulations with mesh size \(h=\max_\tau\operatorname{diam}(\tau)\to0\), and \(u_h\) is the continuous piecewise-affine interpolant of \(u\) on \(\mathcal{T}_h\).

**Theorem 1** (Refinement–resolution bridge). Let \(\mu_h=D^2u_h\) (Definition 1) and \(\mu=D^2u\,\mathrm{dvol}\). Then:

(i) (Atoms / support.) \(\mu_h\) is a finite matrix-valued Radon measure concentrated on the \((p-1)\)-skeleton of \(\mathcal{T}_h\) with atoms \(A_F=[\nabla u_h]_F\otimes n_F|F|\); there are no atoms on lower-dimensional strata.

(ii) (Weak convergence.) \(\mu_h\Rightarrow\mu\) weakly as matrix-valued measures: for every \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)\), \(\langle\mu_h,\varphi\rangle\to\langle\mu,\varphi\rangle\). Moreover \(\|\mu_h-\mu\|_{\mathrm{KR}}\to0\) in the Kantorovich–Rubinstein (flat) norm.

(iii) (Total variation: the exact statement.) \(\sup_h\|\mu_h\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le C\,|u|_{C^2}|\Omega|\) (under quasi-uniformity) and \(\liminf_h\|\mu_h\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ge\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\); but \(\|\mu_h\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\to\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\) is false in general—see Remark 7. It holds in the one-signed regime (convex or concave data restricted to one-dimensional parameter families), which is the regime of the parametric-LP value function and of \(\kappa_\mu\).

(iv) (No-mass-loss, correct form: \(L^1\) reconstruction.) On structured complexes, the per-cell reconstructed density \(\rho_h(\mathrm{cell})=|\mathrm{cell}|^{-1}\sum_{F\to\mathrm{cell}}A_F\) converges to \(D^2u\) strongly in \(L^1\) with rate \(O(h)\) for \(u\in C^3\); consequently \(\|\rho_h\|_{L^1}\to\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\). On unstructured complexes the unweighted three-facet patch is not the dual cell; the general statement requires the discrete-duality (dual-diamond) pairing.

(v) (Resolution window.) For the coarse-grained family \(\mu_{h,\sigma}=\mu_h*\varphi_\sigma\): at fixed \(h\), the limit \(\sigma\to0\) is the atomic measure; the smooth object exists only as a family member at matched resolution through the window \(h\ll\sigma\ll L_{\mathrm{var}}\), where \(L_{\mathrm{var}}\) is the length scale on which the smooth curvature density itself varies (measured in §4.3).

All five parts are proved in Appendix B, the reconstruction part (iv) in Appendix C.

**Remark 7** (Exact counter-example calculus). On the right-triangle mesh of \([0,1]^2\) with Hessian \(H=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\b&c\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\), the per-cell atoms are \(A_{\mathrm{diag}}=bh^2\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top\), \(A_{\mathrm{vert}}=(a-b)h^2\,e_x\otimes e_x\), \(A_{\mathrm{horiz}}=(c-b)h^2\,e_y\otimes e_y\); their sum is exactly \(h^2H\) (machine-verified to \(0.0\)), while the entrywise total variation per cell is \(|a-b|+|c-b|+4|b|\) against the target \(|a|+|c|+2|b|\). Hence for \(u=xy\) the ratio is exactly \(3-1/n\), for the strictly convex \(u=2x^2-xy+2y^2\) it is \(1.4-1/n\) (measured: \(2.9922\) and \(1.3922\) at \(n=128\)), and for generic smooth maps \(3.6\)–\(4.4\). Total-variation convergence fails generically—including under convexity—while weak convergence holds at rate \(O(h^2)\) throughout.

**Proposition 4** (Two-layer separation and the sec law). Let \(K_h=\sum_v\operatorname{defect}(v)\,\delta_v\) be the vertex angle-defect measure of the polyhedral graph \(\{(x,u_h(x))\}\). Then \(K_h\) converges weakly to the Gauss-map area density
\[
\frac{\det D^2u}{(1+|\nabla u|^2)^{3/2}}\,\mathrm{dvol}
=K\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^2}\,\mathrm{dvol},
\qquad
K=\frac{\det D^2u}{(1+|\nabla u|^2)^2},
\]
i.e., curvature per projected parameter area, not the intrinsic Gaussian curvature. The bias factor is exactly \(\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^2}\) (the sec law): machine-verified to \(0.0\%\) excess across \(13\) probes spanning tilts \(0.11\)–\(2.83\) and three tilt directions. Recovering intrinsic curvature requires the graph-area correction.

**Remark 8** (Consequences). (i) The codimension-one Hessian layer is the unbiased estimator and the canonical carrier for the bridge; (ii) Regge/angle-defect formulations need the area correction of Proposition 4; (iii) defects of a fixed complex (as measured in the regime-dial census, §4.3) are scale-invariant \(O(1)\) objects and are not contradicted by Proposition 4, which concerns refinement limits.

**Proposition 5** (Regime dichotomy, in closed form). Let \(B(\varepsilon)\) be the curvature mass captured by a loop of radius \(\varepsilon\). For a \(C^2\) map, \(B(\varepsilon)=O(\varepsilon^2)\); for a piecewise-affine map whose kink complex meets the loop transversally, \(B(\varepsilon)=O(\varepsilon)\). Measured slopes on the regime-dial design (§4.3): \(2.000\) (smooth) and \(1.00\) (parametric FBA, \(76\)-pair perturbation scan).

## 3.1 Bridge to parametric FBA

**Conjecture 1** (mpLP refinement bridge). Let \(\{\Phi_n\}\) be a sequence of parametric-LP value functions (minima of tangent-plane families; \(\theta\) in the right-hand side; nested outer approximations) converging uniformly to a \(C^2\) limit \(f\), with induced complexes \(\mathcal{C}_n\). Then \(\mu_n=D^2\Phi_n\) converges weakly to \(D^2f\,\mathrm{dvol}\) provided the complexes satisfy a per-cell discrete-duality identity (structured complexes) or a stable discrete-duality (dual-diamond) pairing. Along one-dimensional parameter cuts the conclusion holds unconditionally by monotone telescoping of the concave restriction.

**Remark 9** (Status). The one-dimensional cut case is machine-verified (independent-seed reproduction of the refinement experiment: folded Wasserstein distance \(0.058\to0.0039\), mass ratio \(0.939\to1.0009\) across \(n=4\to128\)). The general complex case is open; the discrete-duality pairing stability is the identified missing assumption.

**Remark 10** (Existence versus resolution). Proposition 1 supplies existence of the curvature measure without refinement sequences, smoothing, or interpolation; Theorem 1 governs what a discretely sampled trajectory can resolve of that measure, and at what rate. The two layers are complementary: the Alexandrov layer removes the extrinsic hypotheses, the refinement bridge keeps the quantitative rate.

**Corollary 1** (Decoupling; resolves the value–flux layer relation). Theorem 3 resolves the layer relation exactly: value events are a subset of flux events, never the converse, and the flux events invisible to the value layer are the degenerate reroutings (mask-type events—rerouting without growth loss, the Kochanowski pattern). On the iML1515 cut: \(12\) flux events versus \(1\) value atom, \(11\) objective-invisible; on the reference trajectory (§5.2) the value layer carries no network events at all. The association of \(\kappa_\mu\) with transcriptional response is therefore a flux-layer property by structural necessity, not merely by empirical choice.

# 4. Computational validation

## 4.1 Active-set curvature verification

Thirteen parameter sweeps (glucose and oxygen uptake; capacity knockdowns of ten genes: pgi, zwf, tktA, pfkA, eno, gltA, aceA, ppc, gnd, rpe) on *E. coli* iML1515, solved by a three-stage lexicographic pFBA engine (deterministic optimum; fixed seeded tie-break weights resolving the vertex degeneracy of plain pFBA), with an iJO1366 glucose replication and an aceA unused-pathway negative control.

Result: \(93.4\)–\(100.0\%\) of second-order response mass concentrates on operational active-set switches in every sweep that crosses critical-region boundaries (ten of the eleven crossing sweeps at mass exactly \(1.000000\); mass \(0.934\)–\(1.0\); AUC \(0.83\)–\(1.00\); Mann–Whitney \(p\le2\times10^{-3}\), with seven of eleven crossing sweeps at \(p\le10^{-4}\); Fig. 1); between events the paths are piecewise affine to solver precision (event-free segment residuals \(\le1.2\times10^{-10}\), with \(8\times10^{-14}\) on the glucose sweep; one \(19\)-point segment of the eno sweep carries \(4.1\times10^{-3}\)—a sub-threshold kink invisible to the operational event proxy, which is also why that sweep’s event mass is \(0.934\) rather than \(1.0\)); negative controls behave as theory dictates (unused-pathway knockdown: zero events, zero curvature; single-critical-region sweep: noise-level \(D^2\sim10^{-11}\)).

## 4.2 Double-knockout epistasis and path dependence

\(1{,}516\) single knockouts and \(2{,}779\) double-knockout pairs across five panels. Epistasis \(\varepsilon_{ij}=\kappa_{ij}-\kappa_i-\kappa_j\): all \(40\) synthetic-lethal pairs are isozyme redundancies (tktA/tktB, acnA/acnB, metE/metH, \ldots) with pure-emergence epistasis; \(|\varepsilon_{ij}|\) aligns with active-set footprint overlap (Spearman \(\rho_S=0.865\), \(J_{\mathrm{support}}=0.800\)); sequential (\(L_1\)-MOMA) knockouts open nonzero commutators in \(25\%\) of active pairs; closed genotype loops fail to return to the initial state in \(66\%\) of pairs (phenotypic memory; median holonomy \(\sim110\)).

## 4.3 Regime dial

The smoothing identity \(D^2(v*\phi_\sigma)=(D^2v)*\phi_\sigma\) holds to kernel self-test error \(1.2\times10^{-6}\); measured crossover \(\varepsilon^*/\sigma\in[2.45,4.11]\) (median \(3.1\)) separates the \(O(\varepsilon^2)\) smooth regime from the \(O(\varepsilon)\) discrete regime. The scaling slope is \(1.00\) on the interacting stratum; the regime-dial census resolves the thin-wedge sliver structure (net measure jump \(8.9\) versus nominal \(\pm1884.6\)).

## 4.4 The value function as canonical carrier

\(\Phi=c_{\mathrm{bio}}^\top v^*\) is single-valued with no tie-breaking; it is piecewise affine at \(4.2\times10^{-13}\) on the flux-event partition, carries one real atom \(\Delta\Phi'=-0.006439\) at \(t=0.0358286\), and decouples from the flux-jump hierarchy (value/flux mass ratio \(1.7\times10^{-6}\)). Danskin: finite-difference shadow prices match envelope marginals to \(6\)–\(7\) digits. A controlled follower-variable LP reproduces the same structure: the value function stays affine and identical under two lexicographic tie-breaks while the follower’s second-difference mass flips \(0.0100\leftrightarrow10^{-13}\)—the value layer is canonical (Proposition 1), the flux-strain layer is not (Corollary 1). The coupling identity of Theorem 3 is machine-verified on this same cut: \(\Delta\Phi'=c^\top\Delta v'\) with error \(0.0\) at all \(12\) events and all \(4\) clusters, \(\Phi=c^\top v^*\) to \(10^{-9}\), and the \(11\) invisible events carry flux \(\ell_1\) slope jumps up to \(1.6\times10^4\) against \(|c^\top\Delta v'|\le1.51\times10^{-7}\)—the decoupling is the objective contraction, not a loss of network information.

## 5.4 The layer decision

All value-layer attribution arms, under the declared protocol of §5.3 (iJO1366, the reference physiology, \(n=424\)): the flux layer \(\kappa_\mu\) gives \(r=+0.395\), partial \(r=+0.269\) (\(p=1.8\times10^{-8}\)); the \(c\)-attribution arm is supported on the biomass pseudo-reaction alone (\(0\) of \(433\) genes; Theorem 3(ii)); the shadow-price-jump arm gives \(51\) genes at \(r=+0.032\) (partial \(-0.013\), \(p=0.93\)). The value trajectory has zero chamber crossings: the path stays in one chamber of \(\Phi\) throughout—\(\Phi\) is affine along it, with \(d\mu/dq_{\mathrm{glc}}=Y=0.099544\) (\(=|y_{\mathrm{glc}}|\), the constant glucose uptake shadow price; affine intercept \(-0.0124\)) and uptake shadow prices jumping by exactly zero—all four value kinks are anchor corners of the trajectory design, of magnitude \(Y\Delta(dq_{\mathrm{glc}}/dt)\), carrying no network structure. Value/flux strain mass ratio \(1.45\times10^{-3}\); the coupling identity holds at machine precision (error \(0.0\) at every kink). The value-gated flux strain coincides with \(\kappa_\mu\) exactly (\(433/433\) genes attain their maximum at a value-kink time), a property of the trajectory design (anchor rerouting dominates per-gene maxima), not evidence of value-layer content. The layer decision: the association lives on the flux layer, with Theorem 3 as the exact bridge to the canonical object.

## 5.5 Path robustness: what survives a trajectory change

Three trajectories through the same LP (same engine, seed, panel, statistics; only the path and its matched response vary; Fig. 4): P0 glucose decline (reference-physiology anchors; the layer-decision control of §5.4), P1 oxygen limitation (\(q_{\mathrm{glc}}=5\) fixed, \(q_{O_2}\) linear from \(22\) to \(1\)), P2 acetate switch (\(q_{O_2}=22\) fixed; glucose \(5\to0\) while acetate uptake ramps \(0\to10\)). Kinks are classified by the chamber test—the parameters are the uptake bounds, so \(\nabla\Phi=(y_{\mathrm{glc}},y_{O_2},y_{\mathrm{ac}})\): a kink is a design corner if and only if it sits at a trajectory anchor and the uptake shadow prices are continuous across it (jump \(\le10^{-9}\)), and a chamber crossing if and only if they jump.

(i) The one-chamber property is path-dependent: P0 has zero chamber crossings (the §5.4 control, reproduced digit-exactly), while P1 has three genuine crossings (\(q_{O_2}\approx9.3\), \(6.3\), \(2.9\); \(|\Delta\Phi'|\) up to \(0.68\); \(y_{O_2}\) jumps \(+0.032\) at the respiratory boundary) and P2 has four (two large, near the glucose–acetate handoff; two micro-slivers with dual jumps \(\sim10^{-4}\)). The single-chamber structure of the reference trajectory is a property of the glucose-decline design, not a law.

(ii) The layer decision survives the change: on every path the value-layer arms contribute nothing. The \(c\)-attribution arm is degenerate (\(0\) of \(433\) genes—the sparse-objective corollary of Theorem 3 is path-free); the chamber-crossing shadow-price arm is null (\(r=-0.17\), \(n=68\) on P1; \(r=+0.032\), \(n=71\) on P2); value-gated flux strain is a subset of \(\kappa_\mu\) (equal or weaker on every path, never stronger).

(iii) The flux-layer association generalises: against the matched oxygen response (M3D WT anaerobic versus aerobic contrast) P1 gives \(r=+0.318\) (partial \(+0.258\), \(p=6\times10^{-8}\), \(n=426\)); against the matched acetate-switch response P2 gives \(r=+0.223\) (partial \(+0.160\), \(p=9\times10^{-4}\)); and the P1/P2 predictors correlate with the primary-panel carbon response at \(r=+0.378\)/\(+0.391\)—the per-gene ranking is largely trajectory-independent.

(iv) Theorem 3 holds at every kink on all three paths (identity error \(0\); worst case \(1.6\times10^{-15}\)). Value/flux strain mass ratios: \(1.5\times10^{-3}\) (P0), \(2.4\times10^{-4}\) (P1), \(5.5\times10^{-4}\) (P2).

## 5.6 Tie-break robustness: the association as a property of the protocol class

The flux layer is selection-dependent by construction (Remark 3); the declared lexicographic tie-break is one member of a family. The tie-break experiment fixes the entire protocol of the primary association (iJO1366, reference-physiology anchors, \(8\times\) refinement, the \(433\)-gene panel, the M3D carbon-depletion response, the reference-level confound control) and varies only the stage-3 tie-break of the engine:

- TB0 (declared): \(w\sim U(0.5,1.5)\), seed 20240901, \(\min w^\top v\)—the declared metric (control: reproduces the primary association, \(r=+0.3954\), digit-exactly);
- TB1 (fresh seed): \(w\sim U(0.5,1.5)\), seed 20240902—same family, independent draw;
- TB2 (family swap): \(w\sim\mathrm{LogN}(0,1)\) clipped to \([0.05,20]\), seed 20240903—a different distribution family with a roughly \(130\)-fold wider dynamic range (support ratio \(400:1\) versus \(3:1\));
- TB3 (rule swap): stage-3 objective on the split variables, \(\min\sum_rw_r(f_r+r_r)\)—weighted absolute-flux selection with TB0’s weights;
- TB4 (adversarial): \(\max w^\top v\)—the far end of the stage-2-pinned optimal face, with TB0’s weights.

Three findings (Table 1, Fig. 5). (i) The association is stable: across all five rules \(r\in[+0.386,+0.396]\) (maximum deviation \(0.009\); all \(p\le1.4\times10^{-16}\)) and the partial correlation is constant to three decimals (\(+0.269\)–\(+0.270\)). (ii) The tie-break genuinely binds: the stage-2-pinned optimal face is multi-vertex, and the alternative rules select different flux vectors at all \(57\) grid points (max \(\|v_k-v_0\|_\infty\) up to \(5.0\)), changing per-gene \(\kappa_\mu\) for \(90\)–\(117\) of \(433\) genes—yet the per-gene ranking is essentially unchanged (\(\rho_S\ge0.99897\)), and the changed values are small on the \(\kappa_\mu\) scale (max absolute change \(9.3\times10^{-5}\)); the TB1 deviation of \(0.009\) traces to two boundary genes entering the nonzero support (\(n:424\to426\)), not to a reshaping of the metric. (iii) The value layer is exactly invariant: stage-1 biomass \(\mu\) and stage-2 \(\ell_1\) mass agree across variants to \(0.0\)—Proposition 1 (tie-break-freeness of the value layer) measured on the full trajectory. Together with Corollary 1 this completes the robustness picture: the declared selection is a convention, the value layer does not see it, and the flux-layer association is a property of the lexicographic protocol class, not of one rule.

## 5.7 Event-measure stabilisation (Glivenko–Cantelli type)

The refinement-stabilisation question (*do the empirical event point measures of the lex-pFBA map converge as the sampled family grows?*) has a near-term statistical form, executed here on three data sources: random straight cuts through the \((q_{\mathrm{glc}},q_{O_2})\) signature plane (iML1515, \(34\times34\) census, \(350\) boundary edges; a pool of \(4{,}000\) random cuts yielding \(25{,}107\) cut events), the thirteen parameter sweeps of §4.1 (\(2\) affine, \(11\) event-bearing), and the panel protocol of this paper (gene panel and trajectory). The metric is the bounded-Lipschitz distance (domains normalised to unit diameter, where it equals \(W_1\); exact in one dimension, transport LP in two); each arm carries a permutation null (event locations replaced by uniforms, panel structure kept) and the one-dimensional arms carry the classical Glivenko–Cantelli asymptotic constant \(C=\int\sqrt{F(1-F)}\,dx\) of del Barrio–Ginè–Uzet computed from the population (Fig. 6).

(i) Random panels stabilise at the GC rate. Pooled cut-event locations on the cut coordinate (\(d=1\)): measured tail slope \(-0.492\) versus null \(-0.506\), measured/null ratio \(1.24\) at the largest panel, and the iid prediction \(C/\sqrt n\) matches the measured curve to \(2.5\%\) at the largest panel (mid-range deviations as large as \(8.2\%\)); the two-dimensional pooled locations (transport LP) decay consistently with the \(d=2\) rate up to the reference-noise floor. Sweep panels (the thirteen sweep families) stabilise with a heterogeneity penalty that falls from \(\approx1.8\times\) the uniform null at small panels through \(\approx1.5\times\) at \(k=6\) to \(\approx0.5\times\) at \(k=12\), and a tail faster than iid-GC—consistent with the finite-population correction \(\sqrt{(13-k)/12}\) of sampling without replacement from thirteen heterogeneous sweeps (measured null-relative ratio \(0.49\) at \(k=12\), against the pure FPC value \(0.29\); the residual is the sweep heterogeneity). The gene panel: the \(\kappa\) distribution stabilises at the GC rate (measured effective constant \(\approx1.6\)–\(2.1\) across panel sizes (dipping at \(m=128\)), against the population Glivenko–Cantelli constant \(C=2.49\) on the \(\log_{10}\kappa_\mu\) axis), and the association itself stabilises at \(r\approx+0.39\) with the Fisher standard error \(1/\sqrt{m-3}\).

(ii) Designed panels stabilise exactly, not statistically. The trajectory event point measure is a four-atom object: the four design corners of the physiology anchors carry the entire flux-layer mass (\(288.77\), the primary association’s flux-layer mass to the digit)—the one-chamber structure of §5.4 in its event form. Anchor-preserving thinning reproduces it exactly at every panel size \(m\ge8\) (shape distance \(0\) to machine precision, \(\le7\times10^{-13}\); total-mass error \(\le2\times10^{-12}\); value-kink census \(\equiv4\)); uniform random thinning decays by corner censoring (shape distance \(0.12\to0.005\) by \(m=43\), mass error \(26\%\to0.13\%\) by \(m=29\), machine-zero (\(\le3\times10^{-12}\)) from \(m=43\), and exactly \(0\) at the full panel \(m=57\))—a resolution effect in the sense of Theorem 1(iv), not statistical averaging.

Interpretation: the mean-field intuition is confirmed in its statistical form—the empirical event measures over random panels (cuts, sweeps, genes) are Glivenko–Cantelli objects—and simultaneously sharpened: stabilisation has two regimes, statistical for random panels and structural (exact, design-immediate) for designed panels. The deterministic refinement-sequence form of the question (refinement along explicit model-family sequences) remains open.

## 5.8 Platform-by-class disambiguation (\(2\times2\))

Is the association a carbon-depletion artefact, or a platform artefact? Four cells (two platforms \(\times\) two perturbation classes), executed with the geometric \(\kappa\) metric (rank-equivalent to \(\kappa_\mu\) at \(\rho=0.99998\)): carbon switch on microarray (M3D glycerol/acetate/proline versus glucose) switch-MAX \(r=+0.196\); stress on microarray (heat shock, acid shock, ciprofloxacin) MAX \(r=+0.298\); carbohydrate-responsive transcript levels: carbon starvation on RNA-seq (GSE64021 time course) MAX \(r=+0.191\); heat shock on RNA-seq MAX \(r=+0.345\) (\(n=241\)–\(433\); permutation \(p\le0.003\)). Signed responses flip sign (stress arms \(-0.22\) to \(-0.32\)): the metric tracks rerouting magnitude, not direction, on both platforms and both classes—the association is neither platform-specific nor carbon-class-specific.

## 5.9 Protein abundance versus protein change

Two dissociations at the protein layer (PaxDb integrated abundance; quantitative proteomics of GSE64021; \(n=169\)–\(429\)): (i) abundance *level* associates (PaxDb baseline \(r=+0.334\))—high-rerouting genes are abundant proteins; (ii) abundance *change* does not (protein log-fold change \(r=+0.008\) to \(+0.032\); transcript–protein fold-change coupling \(r=+0.020\), \(n=799\)). The transcriptional association stops at translation, consistent with buffering.

## 5.10 Protein-layer null

Condition-dependent quantitative proteomics (Schmidt et al., 2016; \(22\) conditions, triplicates, \(n=366\)): protein-level correlation \(r=-0.083\) against transcript \(+0.419\) on the same genes. The transcript-level association does not propagate to the protein layer; buffering at translation is the consistent model, positioning Kochanowski et al. (2013) as prior art for the protein-layer null.

# 6. Discussion: one measure, several resolutions

The sensitivity objects of the framework are one measure at different resolutions: the atomic measure of Definition 1, its coarse-grainings \(\mu*\phi_\sigma\), and its integrals along parameter trajectories (\(\kappa_\mu\)). The smooth member exists only through the window \(h\ll\sigma\ll L_{\mathrm{var}}\); the \(\sigma\to0\) limit is the atomic object. Open items: Conjecture 1 (general complex stability) and second differences on measured time courses (under the \((\varepsilon,\sigma)\) design law). The statistical form of the stabilisation question is settled here (§5.7): random panels are Glivenko–Cantelli objects and designed panels reproduce the event measure exactly; the deterministic refinement-sequence form (refinement along explicit model-family sequences) remains open. The exact contraction identity \(D^2\Phi=\sum_rc_r\,D^2v^*_r\) of Theorem 3 grounds the association’s flux-layer footing, complementing the measured metric invariance of §5.3.

**Limitations.** Correlational design; single-organism scope; operational (not combinatorial) active sets; lexicographic tie-breaking is an engine convention (the value-function carrier is immune, the flux layer declares it—Remark 3—and the association is stable across all five selection rules tested (declared plus four variants, §5.6); the one-chamber structure of the reference path is condition-specific (§5.5) while the layer decision itself is path-robust; the cross-platform carbon-switch dissociation (PRECISE arm, §5.3) bounds the generality of the association; the active-reaction census of the reference physiology (\(438/2{,}583\) and the GPR class counts) is plain-FBA and solver-version-sensitive at the \(\pm1\)-reaction level, while the gene-level panel (\(435\)) is stable (Appendix A); the sweep arm of the stabilisation experiment is panel-limited (thirteen sweep families, two of them affine—the finite-population correction is part of the reported result) and the two-dimensional arm is floor-limited at large panels by the transport-LP atom cap.

**The companion theory paper.** The categorical and homotopy-theoretic reading behind the active-set curvature interpretation—the \(2\)-category \(\mathbf{StCon}(B)\) of stratified connections with its gluing theorem, the Fisher-minimal transport law on constant-active-set strata, the optic-composition semantics with its contraction analysis, the filtered-colimit construction of autocatalytic sets, the viability-kernel reading of its dynamical closure test (a finite-time, feedback-certifying probe of kernel and capture-basin membership, with the Poincaré/averaging analysis of its pathwise levels), and the homotopy-type-theoretic extension—is developed in full, as a standalone theory paper with its own proofs, machine verifications, and per-result proof-status marking (the homotopy-type-theoretic extension is proof-sketch-marked there; the stratified gluing is descent-by-citation with construction-level verification), in a companion manuscript. The division of labour is deliberate: this paper is the application—the measure-theoretic curvature of parametric FBA, the metric \(\kappa_\mu\), and the genome-scale empirical association with its layer decision—and carries only a brief adapted statement of the load-bearing definitions (§2.5); the companion carries the framework. Neither manuscript depends on the other’s claims.

**Scope of the contribution.** The mathematics is classical: parametric linear programming (chamber complexes, active sets, Danskin duality), Alexandrov’s theorem, and distributional second derivatives of piecewise-affine maps; Theorem 3 is an elementary identity. The contribution is the systematic application of this machinery to flux-balance models—the two-layer measure structure of the value/flux pair, the exact coupling identity, the signed extension to arbitrary GPR structure—together with the empirical association and its layer decision. No new measure-theoretic theorem is claimed; the nearest prior art (phenotype phase planes, mpLP value-function theory, discrete Monge–Ampère, Regge-type defect measures) is cited where it applies.

# 7. Methods

## 7.1 The lexicographic pFBA engine

All deterministic trajectories are computed by a purpose-built three-stage lexicographic solver on the variable split \(x=(v,f,r)\) with \(v=f-r\), \(f,r\ge0\), stoichiometry \(Sv=0\), and flux bounds \(\ell(\theta)\le v\le u(\theta)\) entering affinely through the uptake bounds. The stages are strict lexicographic optimisations, each pinned at the previous stage’s optimum:
\begin{align*}
(1)&\quad\mu^*=\max\;c_{\mathrm{bio}}^\top v &&\text{(growth)}\\
(2)&\quad s^2=\min\;\textstyle\sum_r(f_r+r_r)\quad\text{s.t. }v_{\mathrm{bio}}\ge\mu^*(1-\varepsilon_\mu) &&\text{(parsimony)}\\
(3)&\quad\min\;w^\top v\quad\text{s.t. }\textstyle\sum_r(f_r+r_r)\le s^2(1+\varepsilon_s) &&\text{(tie-break)}
\end{align*}
with \(\varepsilon_\mu=\varepsilon_s=10^{-9}\) and \(w\sim U(0.5,1.5)\) drawn once with seed 20240901 and fixed thereafter. Stage 3 exists because plain pFBA is vertex-degenerate on these models: repeated warm-started solves of the same LP flip \(0.69\) reactions on average between vertices of the optimal face. The LPs are solved with HiGHS via `scipy.optimize.linprog`, cold-started and deterministic; a presolve-disabled retry handles the rare infeasibility mis-report. The lex optimum exists, is unique by construction, and is continuous piecewise affine in \(\theta\) (Lemma 1); the tie-break is part of the metric’s definition (Remark 3), and its robustness is measured in §5.6. The active-reaction census of the reference physiology is the one plain-FBA computation retained (kept for comparability with the geometric metric \(\kappa\)); it is solver-version-sensitive at the \(\pm1\)-reaction level, while the gene-level panel is stable.

**M3D microarray compendium.** The expression panels come from the M3D *E. coli* compendium v4 Build 6: \(4{,}297\) gene probes \(\times\) \(907\) arrays, \(\log_2\) uniformly normalised. ## 7.3 Panel construction and GPR association

## 7.4 Statistical protocols

The predictor is \(\log_{10}\) of the per-gene \(\kappa_\mu\) (its distribution is log-linear over five decades); the response is \(\max|\log_2\mathrm{FC}|\) of the matched contrast. Reported statistics: Pearson \(r\) with analytic \(p\), Spearman \(\rho_S\) (raw predictor ranks, full panel and nonzero subset as labelled), partial \(r\) controlling the M3D reference level by linear residualisation of both variables, permutation \(p\) (\(10^5\) label permutations, Monte Carlo), bootstrap \(95\%\) CIs (\(10^4\) resamples), and top-versus-bottom decile contrasts (one-sided Mann–Whitney \(U\)). All tests are two-sided except the decile contrast. The PRECISE per-condition arms use the same protocol with TPM log-fold changes. Every manuscript number traces to a deposited artefact file and is re-derived by the automated numeric verification described under Reproducibility (\(98\) checks).

# Appendix B. Proofs

This appendix proves the results of the manuscript in full. Where an argument would repeat textbook material, the standard source is cited in lieu of reproduction; the two long technical arguments (Lemma 1 and the reconstruction part (iv) of Theorem 1) are delegated to Appendix C and cited there.

**Proof of Proposition 1.** Write the primal as \(\max\{c^\top v:Sv=0,\,\ell(\theta)\le v\le u(\theta)\}\) with \(\ell,u\) affine in \(\theta\). Its dual is
\[
\min\bigl\{u(\theta)^\top w^+-\ell(\theta)^\top w^-:S^\top y+w^+-w^-=c,\,w^\pm\ge0\bigr\},
\]
with \(y\) free. The constraint set \(\mathcal{D}\) of the dual does not involve \(\theta\), so by weak duality every dual-feasible triple furnishes an affine upper bound on \(\Phi\), and by strong duality (feasibility and boundedness are hypotheses) the value equals the dual optimum. The dual objective is linear in \((w^+,w^-)\) for each fixed \(\theta\), so the optimum over the polyhedron \(\mathcal{D}\) is attained at one of its finitely many vertices. Hence \(\Phi\) is the pointwise minimum of finitely many affine functions of \(\theta\) and is therefore concave and piecewise affine (the polyhedral value-function structure is classical).

Since \(\Phi\) is concave piecewise affine, \(-\Phi\) is convex piecewise affine, and for every continuous piecewise-affine function the distributional second derivative is the finite signed facet measure computed in the proof of Proposition 2 below; each facet density of \(D^2(-\Phi)\) is positive semidefinite because \(-\Phi\) is convex—the jump of the gradient across a facet of a continuous convex piecewise-affine function is purely normal, \([\nabla(-\Phi)]_F=\alpha_Fn_F\) with \(\alpha_F\ge0\) by monotonicity of the subdifferential. This is the piecewise-affine instance of the Alexandrov Hessian measure of a convex function; in particular \(D^2(-\Phi)\) is a finite symmetric positive-semidefinite matrix-valued Radon measure. As a measure it is a function of \(\Phi\) alone, and \(\Phi\) is a function of the LP data \((c,S,\ell,u)\) alone: no optimal-flux selection rule enters either object. Every measurable selection \(v^*\) of optima reproduces the same \(\Phi=c^\top v^*\), so \(D^2(-\Phi)\) is selection-invariant; the selection acts only on the flux layer \(D^2v^*\), whose mass moves by ten orders of magnitude between two lexicographic tie-breaks while the value layer stays exactly invariant (the controlled follower-variable experiment, §4.1).

For the GPR structure, recall that under multiplicative activity scaling the capacity of a GPR-constrained reaction is built from gene activities by \(\min\) (AND) and \(\max\) (OR) nodes. *AND-only rules:* the capacity \(\min_ka_k(\theta)\,u_j\) is the conjunction of the affine inequalities \(v_j\le a_k(\theta)\,u_j\), one per participating gene, so the feasible set remains a polyhedron with \(\theta\) entering only through affine right-hand sides, and the concavity argument above applies verbatim (AND-only subnetworks retain concavity). *Exchange-bound families:* the bounds are affine in \(\theta\) by assumption; the same argument applies. *Single-gene axes:* hold all activities at the reference level and vary one gene. AND gates fall under the previous case. For an OR gate with further genes held at reference activity, the branch \(\max(a_g(t),1)\,u_j\) is dominated by the constant reference branch for \(a_g(t)\le1\)—the knockdown direction of the empirical trajectories—so the bound is constant; if the varying gene is the gate’s only member, the bound is affine. Either way the axis restriction is a parametric LP with affine or constant bounds, hence concave. *Failure under OR:* an OR gate with two genes, capacity \(\max(\theta_1,\theta_2)\), all else unbounded, gives \(\Phi(\theta_1,\theta_2)=\max(\theta_1,\theta_2)\), which is convex and concave only if affine: at \(\theta_A=(1,0)\) and \(\theta_B=(0,1)\) the midpoint \((\tfrac12,\tfrac12)\) violates concavity by exactly \(\tfrac12\) (the canonical measured violation; the random off-diagonal two-gene probe violates by \(0.548\)). *OR-containing rules:* the value function is the maximum of finitely many concave piecewise-affine functions, one per branch selection, hence continuous piecewise affine, and it carries the signed crease measure of Proposition 6; only the Monge–Ampère layer (Propositions 2 and 3) requires the concave class. \(\square\)

**Proof of Proposition 2.** (i) Let \(\{\tau\}\) be the finite cell decomposition on which \(f\) is affine, with cell gradients \(g_\tau=\nabla f|_\tau\). For a test function \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)\) and indices \(i,j\), the definition of distributional derivatives and a cellwise Green identity give
\[
\langle\partial_i\partial_jf,\varphi\rangle=\int_\Omega f\,\partial_i\partial_j\varphi\,\mathrm{dvol}=-\sum_\tau(g_\tau)_j\int_{\partial\tau}\varphi\,(\nu_\tau)_i\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{p-1},
\]
where \(\nu_\tau\) is the outward normal of \(\tau\); the boundary terms on \(\partial\Omega\) vanish for \(\varphi\) compactly supported. On an interior facet \(F\) shared by cells \(\tau^+,\tau^-\) the two outward normals are opposite, so the facet contributes \(-([g]_F)_j(n_F)_i\int_F\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{p-1}\) with \([g]_F=g_{\tau^+}-g_{\tau^-}\) and \(n_F=\nu_{\tau^+}\); the jump is constant along \(F\) because \(f\) is affine on each cell. Since \(f\) is continuous, the tangential components of the gradient agree across \(F\) (both equal the tangential derivative of the common trace), so \([g]_F\) is purely normal, \([g]_F=[\partial_nf]_Fn_F\), and the facet density \((g^+-g^-)\otimes n_F=[\partial_nf]_Fn_F\otimes n_F\) is symmetric and constant on \(F\). Hence the singular part of \(D^2f\) is the finite facet sum \(\sum_F[\partial_nf]_Fn_F\otimes n_F\,\mathcal{H}^{p-1}|_F\). There are no atoms: each facet \(F\) incident on a codimension-two vertex \(v\) meets \(B_\varepsilon(v)\) in \((p-1)\)-measure at most \(C\varepsilon^{p-1}\) (a ball of \(F\)’s affine hull), and there are finitely many facets, so the total variation mass in \(B_\varepsilon(v)\) is \(O(\varepsilon^{p-1})=O(\varepsilon)\) in the plane—the measured log–log slope \(1.000\).

(ii) For convex \(f\) the Monge–Ampère measure is defined by \(\nu_f(E)=|\partial f(E)|\), the Lebesgue measure of the image of the subdifferential. At an interior cell point the subdifferential is the single gradient; at a facet point it is the segment \(\operatorname{conv}\{g^+,g^-\}\), of area zero; at a codimension-two vertex \(v\) the subdifferential is the convex hull of the gradients of the affine pieces active at \(v\) (the max rule for subdifferentials), which for three or more active pieces in the plane is a two-dimensional polytope—the normal fan of \(v\). Hence \(\nu_f(\{v\})=|\operatorname{conv}\{n_i\}|\), the volume of the normal fan, and these are the only atoms, since facet-level subdifferentials carry zero two-dimensional area. The measured constancy of the atom under \(\varepsilon\)-shrinking of the vertex neighbourhood is the \(\varepsilon\to0\) limit of this identity (\(0.5\) for the canonical right-triangle fan). \(\square\)

**Proof of Proposition 3.** Let \(g=-\Phi\), convex piecewise affine (Proposition 1). The minimum of a linear objective over the dual polyhedron \(\mathcal{D}\) is attained at a vertex, so \(g\) is the maximum of the finitely many affine dual objectives \(C_i(\theta)=-(u(\theta)^\top w_i^+-\ell(\theta)^\top w_i^-)\) indexed by the basic dual solutions \((\lambda_i,y_i,w_i^\pm)\); with the parameters carried into the upper bounds as \(u(\theta)=u_0+U\theta\), the gradient of the \(i\)-th piece is \(\nabla C_i=-U^\top y_i\) (the bound prices \(y_i\) are the sensitivity coefficients of the value function—Danskin’s theorem). The pieces active at \(\theta_v\) are exactly those of the basic dual solutions optimal at \(\theta_v\), and the dual optimal face is the face of \(\mathcal{D}\) on which the \(\theta_v\)-objective attains the optimum, whose vertices are precisely those basic optimal solutions.

By the max rule, \(\partial g(\theta_v)=\operatorname{conv}\{\nabla C_i:i\text{ active}\}\), the normal fan of \(g\) at \(\theta_v\); and by the subdifferential-image formula for the Monge–Ampère measure (proof of Proposition 2(ii)),
\[
\det D^2(-\Phi)(\{\theta_v\})=\nu_g(\{\theta_v\})=|\partial g(\theta_v)|=|\operatorname{conv}\{\nabla C_i\}|,
\]
the area of the normal fan: the first identity of the display. For the second, the linear map \(y\mapsto U^\top y\) sends the dual optimal face to the polytope \(\operatorname{conv}\{U^\top y_i\}=-\operatorname{conv}\{\nabla C_i\}\) (linear maps commute with convex hulls), so the two polygons are reflections of each other through the origin and have equal area:
\[
|\operatorname{conv}\{\nabla C_i\}|=|\{U^\top y:(\lambda,y)\text{ optimal dual}\}|.
\]
The machine verification enumerates the dual optimal face directly: vertex refined to machine precision by the exact triple-tie solve, \(\Phi(\theta_v)\) matching the LP value to \(10^{-15}\), fan area \(0.235\) against dual-face enumeration area \(0.235000068\) over \(16\) direction LPs. \(\square\)

**Proof of Lemma 1.** The proof is delegated to Appendix C: well-definedness of the staged selections by compactness and coordinate pinning; polyhedral chamber structure of each stage by multi-parametric LP theory; polyhedrality of the coordinate-fixing step by Fourier–Motzkin elimination; continuity and piecewise affinity of the inverse of the injective polyhedral projection. \(\square\)

**Proof of Theorem 3.** \(\Phi=c^\top v^*\) holds pointwise by optimality. Both sides are continuous piecewise affine (Lemma 1 for \(v^*\); LP value-function theory for \(\Phi\)), and differentiation of distributions is linear: applying \(D^2\) to the pointwise identity gives the measure identity \(D^2\Phi=\sum_rc_r\,D^2v^*_r\). Restricting to a crease facet \(F\) gives the per-crease form \(\Delta\Phi'(t_k)=c^\top\Delta v'(t_k)\) at every crease time \(t_k\) of any piecewise-affine trajectory, and the contraction bound \(|\Delta\Phi'|\le\|c\|_\infty\|\Delta v'\|_1\) is Hölder’s inequality. (i) An event with unique optima on both sides is a transversal optimal-vertex switch; its slope jump satisfies \(c^\top\Delta v'=\Delta\Phi'\ne0\) unless the value function is affine through it, which would force the optimum to persist—hence objective-invisibility (\(c^\top\Delta v'=0\), \(\Delta v'\ne0\)) occurs exactly inside \(\ge1\)-dimensional optimal faces, where the tie-break selects among vertices without moving the objective; Theorem 3(ii) is the sparse-objective special case. The regularity input (continuity and piecewise affinity of \(v^*\)) is measured, not assumed: segment residuals \(\le8\times10^{-14}\) (§4.1, glucose sweep) and \(\Phi\) piecewise affine at \(4.2\times10^{-13}\) (§4.4). \(\square\)

**Proof of Proposition 6 (collapse).** If \(f\) is \(\lambda\)-semiconvex, then \(D^2f+\lambda I\cdot\mathrm{Leb}\succeq0\) as symmetric-matrix measures. For continuous piecewise-affine \(f\), \(D^2f\) is supported on the codimension-one skeleton, hence purely singular with respect to Lebesgue; testing the inequality on skeleton sets forces the singular part itself PSD, so \(D^2f\succeq0\). Mollifying, \(D^2(f*\rho_\varepsilon)=(D^2f)*\rho_\varepsilon\succeq0\), and \(f*\rho_\varepsilon\to f\) uniformly, so \(f\) is convex as a uniform limit of convex functions. The converse is trivial. The numerical form: the smallest violating scale \(h_{\max}(\lambda)\) of the OR-plus-cap value function satisfies \(h_{\max}=1/(2\lambda)\)—i.e., \(\lambda h_{\max}=1/2\), the measured \(0.500\) identity of the OR-plus-cap value function below—exactly, i.e., no finite semiconvexity constant exists at any scale. The signed-layer statement is elementary distribution theory: a continuous PWL function has distributional \(D^2\) supported on its crease skeleton, a finite symmetric Radon measure with per-crease sign flags. \(\square\)

**Proof of Proposition 7.** At a generic codimension-two vertex of a convex PWL \(f\) on \(\mathbb{R}^2\), exactly three facets meet with gradients \(n_1,n_2,n_3\) (cyclically ordered). The Monge–Ampère atom is the volume of the normal fan \(\operatorname{conv}\{n_i\}\) (the gradient image sweeps the triangle). Writing \(j_i=n_{i+1}-n_i\) for the codimension-one jumps, twice the fan area is \(|\det(j_1,j_2)|\) (the shoelace formula on the triangle), giving atom \(=\tfrac12|\det(j_1,j_2)|=\tfrac12|j_1||j_2|\sin\angle\); the product \(|j_1||j_2|\) overestimates by \(2/\sin\angle\ge2\) with equality if and only if \(j_1\perp j_2\). The simplex-fan generalisation to dimension \(p\) is the same shoelace argument on a \(p\)-simplex fan. Machine verification: \(400\) random max-of-affine vertices, determinant \(=\) fan area to \(8.9\times10^{-16}\), the \(2/\sin\angle\) law to \(2.5\times10^{-12}\), orthogonal edge case exactly \(2\). \(\square\)

**Proof of Theorem 1.** (i) The interpolant \(u_h\) is continuous piecewise affine, so the facet computation of the proof of Proposition 2(i) applies verbatim: \(D^2u_h\) is the finite signed measure \(\sum_F[\nabla u_h]_F\otimes n_F\,\mathcal{H}^{p-1}|_F\) over the interior facets of \(\mathcal{T}_h\), with the jump constant on each facet (hence the atom \(A_F=[\nabla u_h]_F\otimes n_F\,|F|\) of Definition 1, and \([\nabla u_h]_F=[\partial_nu_h]_Fn_F\) by continuity of \(u_h\)). No atoms sit on lower-dimensional strata: the mass in \(B_\varepsilon(v)\) around a point \(v\) of a codimension-two stratum is at most \(\sum_{F\ni v}|[\partial_nu_h]_F|\,\mathcal{H}^{p-1}(F\cap B_\varepsilon(v))=O(\varepsilon^{p-1})\to0\), each facet being a \((p-1)\)-polytope whose intersection with the ball is contained in a \((p-1)\)-ball of radius \(\varepsilon\).

(ii) For \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)\), \(\langle D^2u_h,\varphi\rangle=\int_\Omega u_h\,D^2\varphi\,\mathrm{dvol}\) by the definition of distributional derivatives; no boundary terms arise because \(\varphi\) is compactly supported. The interpolation bound \(\|u-u_h\|_\infty\le\tfrac12 h^2|u|_{C^2}\) holds on shape-regular families by a barycentric argument: for \(x\in\tau\) with vertices \(z_i\) and barycentric coordinates \(\lambda_i(x)\), \(u_h(x)-u(x)=\sum_i\lambda_i(x)(u(z_i)-u(x))\), and Taylor’s formula at \(x\) gives \(u(z_i)-u(x)=\nabla u(x)^\top(z_i-x)+R_i\) with \(|R_i|\le\tfrac12|u|_{C^2}|z_i-x|^2\); the linear terms sum to \(\nabla u(x)^\top(\sum_i\lambda_i(x)z_i-x)=0\) (the barycentre identity), leaving \(\|u_h-u\|_\infty\le\tfrac12 h^2|u|_{C^2}\). Hence \(\int(u_h-u)\,D^2\varphi\to0\) and \(\langle D^2u_h,\varphi\rangle\to\int u\,D^2\varphi=\langle\mu,\varphi\rangle\). For the flat-norm statement, the Kantorovich–Rubinstein norm is the dual norm over the class \(\{\phi:\mathrm{Lip}(\phi)\le1,\|\phi\|_\infty\le1\}\); on the compact \(\Omega\) this class is uniformly equicontinuous and uniformly bounded, hence totally bounded in the uniform norm (Arzelà–Ascoli). Weak convergence holds against each element of a uniform \(\varepsilon\)-net of the class by the first part of this item, and the uniform mass bound of part (iii) controls the remainder of the class, so the supremum over the whole class converges: \(\|\mu_h-\mu\|_{\mathrm{KR}}\to0\), componentwise and hence entrywise. No convergence of total variation is used or needed, consistent with part (iii).

(iii) For the upper bound, the gradient of the linear interpolant satisfies, on each simplex \(\tau\) and for every \(x\in\tau\),
\[
\nabla u_h|_\tau-\nabla u(x)=\sum_iR_i(x)\,\nabla\lambda_i,\qquad|R_i(x)|\le\tfrac12 h^2|u|_{C^2(\tau)},
\]
a decomposition obtained from the same barycentric representation, using \(\sum_i\nabla\lambda_i=0\) and \(\sum_i(z_i-x)\nabla\lambda_i^\top=I\); shape regularity gives \(|\nabla\lambda_i|\le C/h\), so \(\|\nabla u_h-\nabla u\|_{L^\infty(\tau)}\le Ch|u|_{C^2}\). At an interior facet \(F\) with midpoint \(m\), \(|[\nabla u_h]_F|\le2Ch|u|_{C^2}\), and \(|F|\le Ch^{p-1}\); quasi-uniformity bounds the number of facets by \(C|\Omega|h^{-p}\). Summing the entrywise norms of the atoms, \(\|\mu_h\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le C\,|u|_{C^2}|\Omega|\), uniformly in \(h\). For the liminf, the total variation of a matrix-valued measure is the sum of the total variations of its entries, each lower semicontinuous under weak convergence, so \(\liminf_h\|\mu_h\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ge\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\). That convergence of the total variation itself fails in general is the content of Remark 7 (exact ratios \(3-1/n\) and \(1.4-1/n\); generic smooth maps \(3.6\)–\(4.4\), including convex data). In the one-signed regime along a one-dimensional family the telescoping identity restores it: for convex \(u\in C^2\) on \([a,b]\) with mesh \(a=t_0<\dots<t_N=b\), the interpolant \(u_h\) is convex, its slope process \(u_h'\) is nondecreasing piecewise constant, and
\[
\|\mu_h\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=\mu_h([a,b])=\sum_k\Delta u_h'(t_k)=u_h'(b)-u_h'(a)\to u'(b)-u'(a)=\mu([a,b])=\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}},
\]
the endpoint slopes being one-sided secant slopes of \(u\); the concave case is identical up to sign. The parametric-LP value function is concave, so its one-dimensional restrictions sit in this regime, as does \(\kappa_\mu\) along a \(C^1\) trajectory.

(iv) The reconstruction statement is proved in Appendix C (quadratic exactness of the per-cell facet sum on structured complexes, a third-order Taylor remainder bound, and the \(L^1\) summation over cells).

(v) At fixed \(h\), \(\mu_h\) is a fixed finite facet sum; for the mollified family and a test \(\psi\), \(\int\psi\,\mathrm{d}(\mu_h*\varphi_\sigma)=\int(\psi*\tilde\varphi_\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_h\to\int\psi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_h\) as \(\sigma\to0\), because \(\psi*\tilde\varphi_\sigma\to\psi\) uniformly and \(\mu_h\) is a finite measure: the limit is the atomic measure. For the window, fix \(\sigma\) and let \(h\to0\) first:
\[
\mu_{h,\sigma}(x)=\langle\mu_h,\varphi_\sigma(x-\cdot)\rangle\to\langle\mu,\varphi_\sigma(x-\cdot)\rangle=(\mu*\varphi_\sigma)(x),
\]
uniformly in \(x\) over compact sets, by the weak convergence of part (ii) applied to the family of translates \(\{\varphi_\sigma(x-\cdot)\}\), which is uniformly bounded and equicontinuous in \(C^2\), together with the uniform mass bound of part (iii). The second limit is classical mollification of the smooth density: \(\|\mu*\varphi_\sigma-D^2u\|_{L^1}\le C\sigma|u|_{C^3}\). Combining the two: at matched resolution, with \(h/\sigma\) small enough that the first error is dominated and \(\sigma/L_{\mathrm{var}}\) small enough that the second is, the coarse-grained family \(\mu_{h,\sigma}\) tracks the smooth curvature density, while for \(\sigma\ll h\) it resolves the individual atoms and for \(\sigma\gtrsim L_{\mathrm{var}}\) it averages out the very variation it is supposed to measure: the smooth object exists only inside the window \(h\ll\sigma\ll L_{\mathrm{var}}\), as measured in §4.3. \(\square\)

**Proof of Proposition 4.** The proof combines three facts. (a) Exact polyhedral identity. For a polyhedral surface, the angle defect at a vertex equals the area of the spherical image of its normal fan: transporting the incident facet normals to the unit sphere, the spherical polygon they span has area \(2\pi\) minus the sum of the exterior angles at the vertex, which is the defect; the identity is machine-verified to \(0.0\) on the canonical fan (defect, spherical excess, and fan integral all equal). (b) Smooth graph identity. For the graph \(S_u=\{(x,u(x))\}\), the Gauss map \(n(x)=(-\nabla u(x),1)/\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^2}\) has spherical Jacobian per unit *projected* (parameter) area
\[
|\operatorname{Jac}n|=K\frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{dvol}}=\frac{\det D^2u}{(1+|\nabla u|^2)^{3/2}},
\]
because the Jacobian with respect to *surface* area is the Gaussian curvature \(K=\det D^2u/(1+|\nabla u|^2)^2\) (the determinant of the shape operator, the classical Gauss-map identity) and the surface element is \(\mathrm{d}A=\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^2}\,\mathrm{dvol}\). (c) Convergence. The vertex defect measure of the interpolant’s graph assigns to each parameter-domain vertex the spherical area of its normal fan; by (a) this is the polyhedral Gauss-map image, and the curvature measures of piecewise-flat approximations converge weakly to the smooth curvature measures. On the graph of \(u_h\) over the triangulated parameter domain, the facet normals track the smooth Gauss map at scale \(h\) (the gradient interpolation bound \(\|\nabla u_h-\nabla u\|_\infty\le Ch|u|_{C^2}\) of the proof of Theorem 1(iii)), so the union of the vertex normal fans over a parameter region \(R\) covers the spherical image \(n(R)\) up to a vanishing boundary discrepancy. Combining (a)–(c), against a test \(\psi\) on the parameter domain,
\[
\int\psi\,\mathrm{d}K_h\to\int\psi(x)\frac{\det D^2u(x)}{(1+|\nabla u(x)|^2)^{3/2}}\,\mathrm{dvol},
\]
the Gauss-map area density per projected parameter area. The *intrinsic* Gaussian curvature is the same integrand divided by \(\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^2}\); the discrepancy factor is exactly \(\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^2}=\sec(\mathrm{tilt})\), the tilt being the angle between the graph normal and the vertical: the sec law. Recovering intrinsic curvature therefore requires multiplying by the graph-area correction, as stated. The \(0.0\%\) excess across the \(13\) probes spanning tilts \(0.11\)–\(2.83\) and three tilt directions is the numerical form of the exact factor. \(\square\)
