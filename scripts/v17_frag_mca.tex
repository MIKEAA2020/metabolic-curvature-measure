\subsection{Relation to metabolic control analysis}\label{sec:mca}

Metabolic control analysis \citep{kacser1973, heinrich1974} asks a
sister question with different machinery: how much does a
steady state move when one enzyme's kinetics change? Its answers
--- flux and concentration control coefficients, elasticity
coefficients --- are \emph{local smooth sensitivities}: first
derivatives at a single stable steady state of a kinetic model,
defined exactly where the response is differentiable. The objects
of this paper are their complements. $\kmu$ is a global,
path-integrated, second-order measure concentrated precisely where
differentiability fails --- on active-set boundaries --- and it
needs only stoichiometry and bounds, not kinetic parameters. The
two frameworks meet in the middle of the resolution window: the
$\sigma$-smoothed curvature densities of Theorem~\ref{thm:Bprime}
are the regime in which smooth sensitivity language is valid, and
there the averaged discrete measure plays the role that control
analysis assigns to smooth response coefficients. Outside the
window --- at a wall --- the steady state is not unique or the
response is kinked, control coefficients are undefined, and the
discrete measure is exactly at home.

The structural analogies are exact enough to be worth naming. The
flux-control summation theorem ($\sum_i C_i = 1$) says that the
system-level view sees a fixed contraction of the enzyme layer;
the coupling identity $D^2\Phi = \sum_r c_r\,D^2 v^*_r$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:coupling}) is the parametric-LP form of the same
statement, and the invisible events of
Theorem~\ref{thm:coupling}(i) are the degenerate faces where a
control coefficient would have to divide its response among
infinitely many co-optimal states. The empirical dissociation adds
a sharper point: control analysis lives at the enzyme level ---
kinetic parameters set the response --- whereas the transcriptional
association here lives on rerouting geometry that the protein
layer does not carry (\S\ref{sec:e27}). Regulation tracks the
geometry of the option set, not the kinetic magnitudes of the
enzymes that implement it.
